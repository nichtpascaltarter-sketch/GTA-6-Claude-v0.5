Terrain baked into data for the hand-built world; walkers turn at a pace their feet can follow; feet pivot where they touch

The first step of replacing the procedural world with a hand-built one (docs/HANDBUILT_WORLD.md): the terrain - the
only thing the new world keeps - is baked once from today's generator into data files. Nothing in the game reads them
yet; the procedural world is unchanged.

- world/terrain/: the first NTW file (terrain.ntw: the grid, its layers and their hashes, the water levels, the
  coast, island and lake outlines and the channels) and four compressed grids: heights in cm (2.30 MB), the water
  class, today's region map and the coast distance (2.65 MB in all). Natural terrain plus Port Isle's quay walls,
  ring and berth and the Key Coral golf pond; no roads, pads or site grading.
- src/world/gridcodec.cpp/.h: a lossless grid codec (median prediction, adaptive Golomb-Rice residuals, run mode;
  bit-exact, portable, a hash per grid). Heights pack to 17.6% of raw.
- tools/terrainbake.cpp: the host tool that writes world/terrain/ and decodes every grid back from disk to check it.
- src/world/sites.cpp/.h: gTerrainBakeLandforms (false in the game): with it, the site layout runs only the layouts
  that shape land and water outside pads.

Checks (city agent):
- Codec unit tests, 44 cases (every size 1x1-5x4, extremes, smooth terrain, class maps, random data, bad arguments):
  all round-trip; 200 random corruptions per case rejected or decoded without a crash; truncated streams refused.
- The bake: 8.8 s; the round trip from disk matches.
- Procedural world unchanged: worldcheck on all cells, colliders, props, overlaps and beacons is identical to before.
- clang MSVC-safety scan: 0 diagnostics. MinGW syntax of the game: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

Animation (src/anim/animator.cpp): a planted foot twisted further than the leg turns (quick turns) now pivots where it
touches the ground - on its heel at a heel strike, else on the ball, or on the toe tip if it has rolled onto it. The
foot is rolled heel-up about that contact before the contact, the pin onto the ground and the IK are worked out;
before, the 0.2 rad heel lift came after the pin, so the whole foot floated 2-4 cm while it pivoted.
- Harness: planted-foot float frames turnstart.180 34 -> 4, corner.90 74 -> 32, shuffle 21 -> 2, turnwalk.180 35 -> 10;
  slides about level. anim_test: ALL PASSED.
- In-game locotour: slide per step unchanged (mean 0.82 -> 0.84 cm, p90 1.59), scuffs 50 -> 42, planted floats over
  15 mm 114 -> 110. Locostairs unchanged.

AI set 18 (src/game: ai.cpp, ai_game.h, pedai.cpp, police.cpp; app.cpp test modes only):
- Walkers turn at a rate a gait can follow (ai_turn): 4 rad/s standing or barely moving, 2.4 rad/s from 0.4 m/s up, so
  a right angle takes a step or two; walking, the step stays within 35 degrees of the body's facing (an arc, not a
  crab), and the pace eases with the turn's size, so a turn-round is taken almost on the spot. Used by pedai's walkers,
  BRAIN_FOLLOW / BRAIN_GOTO and police on foot (6-9 rad/s before). Runs, aiming and fights keep their quick turns.
- A BRAIN_GOTO walker behind someone slower keeps their pace a little way back, and steps round one who has stopped.
- A walking companion's "stopped with the leader / walking along" choice has hysteresis and a 0.8 s minimum hold, so
  it no longer flips frame to frame (which restarted the upper-body crossfade every flip).
- Test modes gotofollow and turntest; the parking mode honours --time.
- Checks (AI agent, under Wine): gotofollow, turntest, knock, crossing, crowded, copreturn, walkby all PASS (crossing
  and walkby identical to the baseline). MinGW -Wall syntax check clean on HEAD + these five files.
