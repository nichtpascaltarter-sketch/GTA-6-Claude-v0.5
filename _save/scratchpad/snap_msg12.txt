Junctions without rain stripes, smoke that glows when backlit, sharper wet edges under roofs, cleaner materials

- Roads: lane wheel tracks, the oil band, lane-joint cracks and the kerb furniture (gutter, drains, manholes) now
  apply only to road segments. On junctions, turning bulbs and lots their world-aligned tracks repeated every 3.5 m
  into glossy stripes across the junction in the rain; those surfaces get broad polished areas and scattered oil
  stains instead, and a street lamp's reflection on a wet junction is one clean streak.
- Smoke and spray: lights are chosen by how much of their beam reaches the puffs alive now, and local lights scatter
  forward (Henyey-Greenstein), so smoke with a light behind it glows.
- Wet and dry edges under roofs and awnings: the sky-exposure test filters over the overhead map's texels instead of
  point-sampling them, so the line between dry and wet ground is no longer stair-stepped.
- Materials: concrete pores, sand specks and metal scratches fade to their average where the texture can't resolve
  them (no magnified squares at small material sizes), and grain, brushed metal, planks and rain streaks tile
  without a seam.
- Reflections: a screen-space hit must be reached by a ray travelling into the surface.
- Debug views write to their own texture, so the lit image keeps feeding TAA, exposure and the colour pyramid
  (debug view 13 no longer reflects itself).
- Tests: the police search test hides 200 m away inside the search area and follows an officer working the search
  plan; the car door choreography gets more authoring (still not used by the game).
- Test rig: games wait for 3 GB free under the memory cap (D3D12 games use about 2.5-2.7 GB under vkd3d).
