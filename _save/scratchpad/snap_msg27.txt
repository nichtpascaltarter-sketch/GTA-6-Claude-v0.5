City batch 3: motels, strip malls, farms and neighbourhood churches in real types; a lighter far LOD

- Motels come in four types instead of one box:
  - fifties MiMo motor courts: eave slab, steel walkway with coloured panels, boomerang pylon sign;
  - Mediterranean motels: barrel-tile roofs, stucco piers and parapet, blade sign;
  - Keys motels in wood and tin, with a two-level wooden gallery;
  - seventies motor inns: shingled mansard, pole sign.
  All have lobby ends, back wings and sign towers, and Key Solano's side streets no longer look stamped.
- Strip malls come in three types:
  - mission-revival strips: tile-roofed arcade on stucco piers, entry tower;
  - MiMo strips: folded-plate canopy on pipe columns, pylon sign;
  - power centres: tall parapet, anchor end, metal canopy.
  Canopy supports stand on the stall lines, clear of parked cars.
- Farms in Redland and the farmlands:
  - Florida cracker farmhouses (raised, deep porch, tin roof), two-storey I-houses and ranch farmhouses;
  - gambrel barns with X-braced doors, open pole barns, and gable barns with lean-to sheds.
- Neighbourhood churches on the boulevards and in the small towns: mission revival (espadana gable with a bell,
  corner bell tower), white clapboard with a steeple, brick Gothic with a pinnacled tower, and sixties A-frames with a
  glazed front and a bell pylon. The church envelope and front are unchanged, so the bride encounter's chapel stop is
  unaffected.
