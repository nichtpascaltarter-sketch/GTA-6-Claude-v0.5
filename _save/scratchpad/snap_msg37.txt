Bungalows, foursquares, mobile homes, Keys motels and filled-in frontage; eye sockets closed at the sides

City batches 6, 8 and 9 (src/world: buildings.h/.cpp, buildmesh.cpp, facadedetail.cpp, cellgen.cpp, blockstyle.cpp,
massing.cpp, sitegeo.cpp) and a street-name fix (roads.cpp):
- House forms (batch 9):
  - Bungalows come in several types: Craftsman side-gabled ones with the roof running down over a full-width porch and a
    dormer on top, hipped bungalows with a hipped dormer, and "airplane" bungalows with a small windowed room astride
    the ridge. In the older city there are also stucco Mission bungalows with a flat roof, a curved gable and a loggia.
  - American Foursquares in the towns and some in the city: square two-storey houses with a pyramid roof, a hipped
    dormer and a porch across the front, in brick, board or stucco.
  - Craftsman porches have square columns on brick or stone piers with knee walls; town Craftsman houses get earth-tone
    siding with cream trim. Porches stand over the front door, with the rail open at the door.
- The Keys and the towns (batch 6):
  - Two in five Keys motels have a tin hip roof, with gallery rails painted white or turquoise, sea green, coral or
    butter yellow.
  - Porch posts, rails, brackets, gallery ceilings and gable trusses on town houses take the house's trim colour
    instead of bare brown wood; raised Keys houses get painted deck rails.
  - Eyebrow houses: on most two-storey conch houses the front eave comes down over the upper windows.
  - A third of ranch houses and a fifth of block houses have a side-gable roof instead of a hip.
  - A quarter of the Mediterranean houses have a flat roof behind a stucco parapet with a barrel-tile pent.
- Bare frontage filled in (batch 8): along the streets of the towns and the older city, the open ground between houses
  becomes vacant lots (rough grass, bare earth, an old slab, a FOR SALE board, scrub), small street parking lots with
  painted stalls, a lamp, a low wall and a P sign, side yards with fences, and paved yards on the industrial streets.
- Mobile homes in Ten Palms, the Keys, the towns and on the farms: single- and double-wides on lattice or block skirts,
  with decks, awnings and carports.
- Conch houses: temple-front houses with a gable-fronted bay, and L-shaped houses whose porch sits in the inner corner
  (it used to float in front of the set-back part). Some Keys motels get a lower office end. Gallery ceilings are
  painted, some in haint blue.
- The Grove's east-west streets get proper suffixes (Street, Avenue, Terrace...). Their suffix index went negative, so
  most read a word from before the suffix table (an out-of-bounds read: "Coral Gulfstream").
