City batch 4: block houses, folk Victorians, raised Keys houses, four gas stations; yard trees out of houses

- New house types:
  - Fifties Florida block houses: pastel stucco, a low hip roof, aluminium awnings and a carport. They stand among
    the bungalows of North Porto Sol, Calle Luna, the Flats and Midtown, and in Westbrook, the small towns and the
    Keys.
  - Folk Victorians: two-storey frame houses with a cross gable, a bracketed porch in the inner corner and a gable
    truss. They are in Okahatchee, Harlow, Fort Castell, Key Solano and among the farmhouses.
  - Raised Keys houses in Key Solano, Ten Palms and the Keys: the living floor a storey up on concrete piers, open
    parking with storage behind, a front deck with a stair, a metal roof and storm shutters.
- Carports replace the garage wing on block and MiMo houses: a flat roof on steel posts, some with a screen of fins.
- More house shapes:
  - ranch and block houses with a gable-fronted bay forward of the front;
  - L-shaped bungalows with the porch in the corner;
  - fewer plain villas on Key Coral and the bay islands.
- Gas stations in four designs:
  - a flat canopy with a lit brand band;
  - a sixties butterfly canopy on tapered columns;
  - an old service station with a barrel-tile hip roof;
  - a slab carried out from the kiosk.
  All have pump islands, and most a pole price sign at the corner.
- Roofs: tile in three blends (terracotta, brown, aged dark red) and white roofs on some ranch houses. Westbrook no
  longer reads as one orange sheet from the air.
- Yard trees are placed in the front and back yards measured from the house. Of 47,580 yard trees, 5,357 stood inside
  a building, porch, pool, carport or driveway; none do now.
- Strip-mall and gas-station forecourts follow the ground instead of a slab floating 0.35 m above it, so cars and
  shoppers no longer sink into it.
  - A 1 m curbed walk runs along the shopfronts.
  - Where a building sits at an embankment road's level, the forecourt is a solid plateau (9 of 834).
