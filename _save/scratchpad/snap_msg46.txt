Night reads as night, stair landings on target, and buzz cuts that read as short hair

Night renderer phase B (src/shaders/post.hlsl, src/render/post.cpp, src/shaders/skycommon.hlsli, src/shaders/lighting.hlsl,
src/render/renderer.cpp, PROGRESS.md):
- Exposure: at night the exposure no longer opens up beyond EV 1.8. A moonlit suburb, a park or the city from the air
  stays dark instead of being lifted to a dusk grey. Lamp pools, shop fronts and lit rooms still set the exposure where
  they fill the frame.
- Night vision: at moonlight levels colours fade and shift towards blue (reds darken first). Lamp pools, signs and lit
  rooms keep their colour. The warm daytime grade turns neutral at night.
- Sky:
  - The moon is a bright disc with a soft halo in the haze.
  - About 3,000 stars, drawn as pixel-wide points over five magnitudes, fade under the city's light pollution, near the
    moon and low in the haze.
  - The natural night sky is a deeper blue.
- Unlit corners near the camera stay dark: the bounce light gathered around the camera is weaker at night and reaches
  80 m instead of 280 m.
- Backlit grass no longer glows in moonlight.
- Daytime is unchanged: every change is gated on night or the moon.

Animation: landing predictions, the in-game ascent overshoot (src/anim/animator.cpp; src/anim/character.h adds one
Animator member, rootRatio):
- strikeLead (the learned lead of the heel strike, used by every landing prediction) was read at the plant update, an
  update or two after the clip's strike. By then the stance heel had gone back by the body's travel, so every landing fell
  short by that much: 2.5 cm at 30 Hz, up to 8 cm for walkers whose plants come late. That travel is now added back.
- rootRatio: the share of the gait's travel the body actually covers (an EMA over 0.05 s) scales the remaining travel in
  both landing predictions, so a walker pressed against someone no longer lands long.
- Down a flight, the heel keeps 4 cm more off the riser behind.

Faces batch 8: buzz cuts (src/anim/hair.cpp, tests/anim/anim_test.cpp):
- Buzz cuts look like short hair now, not a smooth, shiny swim cap. Up close there is a fine grain with a soft outline and
  hairline. Under a street lamp the top shows a fine sprinkle of highlights instead of a satin band.
- Faded buzz cuts get shorter hairs down the clippered sides, and none where they are taken to the skin.
- How: buzz cuts had only the 2.5 mm shell. They now get scalp cards like the other styles, tuned for short hair:
  - many small one-segment cards, 4-6 mm long;
  - each card's direction scattered about the crown-whorl flow;
  - the cards set deeper in the hair volume, with freshly cut tips;
  - the hairline's fine hairs at half length and 0.6x density.
- Nothing else changes: other hairstyles, hair under hats, the protagonists and the crowd LODs look as before. From 15 m
  buzz cuts look as before.
- Cost: a buzz cut now carries the 2.4-2.7k card triangles a short cut always has. No extra draw calls (the cards join
  the brow and lash card range).
