WHAT (player-facing)
On spiral stairs and winders, people keep their feet on the treads (shown in the harness and the building-kit sweeps; my in-game spiral is already clean with c14, see TESTS). Going down a spiral, the inner foot no longer lands on the narrow end of the wedge treads, where it used to stand a third inside the tread below. Going up winders, a foot meeting a tread too short for it keeps its ball on that tread instead of dropping it into the riser. Sprinting up a straight flight, the player's swing foot no longer drags across the top landing (a 58 cm slide in game).

HOW (animator.cpp)
- Going down round a bend on stairs (0.5 /m or tighter), the inner foot's landing moves out from the bend's centre by 3 cm per 1/m of curvature, at most 7 cm, toward where the wedge treads are deeper. The turn rate is smoothed over 0.6 s first, so a weave down a straight flight doesn't count. The move is kept with the world, scanned with the landing and cleared with the fit.
- Going up, a tread shorter than the foot along its way (a winder's narrow end) is fitted with the ball on it, the toes nearer the riser ahead, instead of with the toes short of the riser and the ball off the tread. This holds only for a tread at or above where the foot was to land, so it never picks the far side of a turn going down. The same rule catches the top of a straight flight at a sprint, where the foot used to skim the landing.
