WHAT (player-facing)
At a switchback's 180-degree turn (every hand-built stairwell), the player running round it used to put the outer foot into the wall: the whole foot past the wall face, sunk 20-30 cm into the floor. Now that foot lands on the landing next to the wall. Running up and down the switchback, feet also sink into the steps less (numbers below). Walking, and anything with no wall or drop next to the feet, is unchanged.

HOW (animator.cpp)
- The game's probes under a wall read the street below it (the wall's top is out of reach). The ground scan ahead of a landing (12 samples, 5 cm apart) now marks such samples as "no ground": as low as the scan reads (1 m) and nearer than the steepest stair would allow, or past a drop of 40 cm or more between two neighbouring samples whose low side is out of a stair's reach. No stair has either. A landing next to one keeps its toes short of it ahead, and its heel past it behind (moves of up to 0.45 m instead of 0.2).
- A swing whose landing is found over no ground is pulled in toward where the body will be when it lands, 0.3 of the way each time it is found, at most 0.6 m. It is found by that scan, or by its landing probe reading the game's clamp: within 0.3 m of the body, or past that where the scan found it too. The pull only acts on a fresh finding (the landing still within 10 cm of where it was found): a body stopped by the wall brings a landing that was predicted past it back by itself.
- While a swing is pulled, every other landing probe goes where it would land without the pull. When that one finds ground, the pull halves.
- A probe taken as no ground is not used as the ground. That's what used to sink the foot toward the street under the wall.
