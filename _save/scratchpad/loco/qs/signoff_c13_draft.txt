WHAT (player-facing)
The player, and anyone jogging or running, now keeps their feet on the treads going up or down stairs. Before this, running up the metro stairs (4 m/s) a third of the player's planted feet stood inside a step, sprinting up half of them, and sprinting down half the planted frames floated above the treads. Also, people walking a switchback at the stair pace (0.5 m/s) no longer put a forefoot inside the first riser of the next flight after the turn on the landing.

HOW (animator.cpp)
- Landing predictions at a jog or a run use the run's own heel-strike lead, learned in metres (about 0.3 m at any run pace). The walk's lead is 0.3 of a stride, which at a run put the landings 0.4-0.6 m too far and off their treads. The two blend between 2 and 3 m/s. Walking is unchanged.
- On stairs and their landings: a foot whose stance is over, which the slow walk's low swing still holds down and which the turning body has taken to the leg's reach, goes to the gait's swing (its landing scanned and fitted to a tread) instead of a quick step. The quick step used to end inside that swing, get lifted again at once, and come down unfitted. This applies in the first half of the swing only; later, the early touchdown would plant the foot again at once and the two would alternate every frame (repeated footstep sounds).
