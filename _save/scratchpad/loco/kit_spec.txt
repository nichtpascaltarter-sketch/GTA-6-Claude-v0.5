Building kit: stair and door geometry the walk animation handles well
(locomotion harness, 6 body types, the stair-pace fix in, peds at the stair pace the AI will use: 0.5-0.6 m/s along the
flight. "Sunk" = share of planted-foot frames with the sole 3 cm or more inside a step.)

- Pace: on stairs peds walk 0.45-0.6 m/s along the flight, one tread a step. At the flat pace (1.2-1.4 m/s) they take
  two or three treads a step.
- Straight flights and porch steps: riser 0.15-0.22 m (all equally fine), tread 0.26-0.33 m (sunk 0-1.1 %).
  Best 0.17-0.18 x 0.28-0.30. Tread 0.24 m: the forefoot hangs over the nose going down (4.5 %). A level landing at
  least 0.9 m deep in front of a door.
- Switchbacks (180-degree landing): flights 0.9-1.2 m wide with landings 0.8-1.4 m deep are all fine (mean 1.1 %,
  worst 4.4 %). Keep the landing at least as deep as the flight is wide, and at least 0.9 m.
- L stairs (90-degree landing): 0.9-1.0 m flights and landings are best (1.2 %); 0.8 m or 1.2 m give 3-3.5 %.
- Winders: three 30-degree winders need the walking line 0.6 m from the inner corner, i.e. a flight at least 1.2 m
  wide (2.4 %). With the walking line at 0.4-0.5 m, 4-7 %. In stairs people use a lot, prefer a landing to winders.
- Spirals: at least 0.28 m of tread at the walking line, and the walking line at least 0.55 m from the centre. That
  means 30-degree treads at 0.55 m (up 0.2 %, down 3.9 %), 26 degrees at 0.6 m (2-8 %), 22.5 degrees at 0.7 m or more.
  So 1.6-2.0 m diameter, walking line 0.55-0.6 m out, 26-30 degrees a tread; risers 0.19 and 0.22 m behave the same.
  Avoid walking lines under 0.5 m or under 0.25 m of tread there: 7-40 % of planted frames end up inside a riser.
- Curved stairs: radius 2 m or more behaves like a straight flight (0-1 %); 1-1.5 m is acceptable (3 % going down).
- Doors and turns: walking straight, the hands stay within 0.34 m of the body line and the feet within 0.18 m; in a
  turn the hands reach 0.38 m. So doors at least 0.8 m clear (a centred walker has 6 cm a side), 0.9 m where the path
  turns right behind the door. A ped turning round on the spot steps within a circle about 0.85 m across: corridors and
  landings need about 1.0 m to turn round in.
- Escalators: not walked by the animation. Model the ride as a moving floor the ped stands on, with no walking speed
  passed to the animator while riding.
