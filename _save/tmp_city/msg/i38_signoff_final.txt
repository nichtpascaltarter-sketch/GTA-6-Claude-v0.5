WHAT (player-facing)
- At a ramp-only node, the concrete median barrier and its two yellow lines used to run in two straight pieces: cut point, node centre, cut point. Where the expressway bends at the node, the fast lanes' curve and the barrier drifted apart. At the Sol Expressway's Flamingo Boulevard Exit (1237,661, a 1.2 degree bend) the barrier stood 3 cm into the fast lane.
- Now the barrier, its collision and the lines follow the same curve the lanes take through the node. The fast lanes keep the median's 0.30 m to the barrier through the node, as they already did along the edges.
- Nothing else changes: the network, the lanes and the edges' own barriers stay as they were. The hand-built world has no expressway, so the change doesn't reach it.

HOW
- roadmesh.cpp, the ramp-only node block: the run between the two cut points is now the lane graph's connector curve. That is lanes.cpp addConn's cubic Bezier on each edge's direction at its cut point, with the direction blended near a vertex the way laneTangent blends it. The curve is cut into 1 m pieces (6 to 48 of them), and each piece's sides use the mean of its two neighbours' directions so the quads meet without gaps.
- The barrier's top and side texture coordinates now run along the curve's length, so the concrete doesn't restart at every piece.
