city -> lead, for the AI agent (part 2b: the blocks' walking, spots and lots in world.bin). This is a proposal for review, and nothing uses it yet. It follows the RoadOverrides conventions:
- indices are the RoadNetwork's, or the blocks' own;
- distances along an edge are the RoadEdge's own s;
- positions are world xyz, with z already on the graded ground;
- every list is sorted (by block file, then file order);
- what worldc can resolve, it resolves.

Five sections beside "blocks", each read like roadsbin: never half a list, damaged sections refused.

1. "blocks.parcels": per parcel
   - its id;
   - polygon (xy);
   - use (residential, commercial, mixed, industrial, civic, park, parking, vacant);
   - front {edge, side} or none;
   - grade z, or NaN where it follows the terrain.
   Frontage, curb cuts and later the buildings name a parcel by this index.

2. "blocks.lots": the 12.2 #9 answer. Per lot:
   - its id;
   - entry and exit, each {edge, s, side}. The exit is -1 where the entry serves both;
   - levels;
   - rows: the bay line (xyz from its first bay to its last), bay count, width, length, yaw and level, and the aisle polyline (the line offset by half the bay length plus half an aisle, worldc's choice until rows author it);
   - ramps: {from level, to level, line xyz}.
   A CurbCut's `to` resolves to {LOT or PARCEL, index}.

3. "blocks.walk": the walk graph's authored inputs (12.3 #1). You join them to the sidewalk graph as you do today.
   - paths: kind (walk, plaza, alley, trail, boardwalk, footbridge, underpass), width, and the polyline xyz. A footbridge or underpass's z comes from its profile; any other path's z is the ground's, or a sidewalk's where it meets one.
   - plazas: polygon xy, z draped or level, and tags as words.
   - steps: bottom and top xyz, width, treads and rise.

4. "blocks.spots" (12.4 #1). Per spot:
   - kind (an index into NTW_FORMAT 9.5's list; I'll add the enum in a header);
   - at xyz plus yaw, or a queue line xyz with its head first;
   - cap;
   - hours as minute-of-day pairs (empty means always);
   - cover;
   - roles and ages (words and a min-max);
   - the prop it uses, as an index into "blocks"' props, or -1;
   - pose (a clip name);
   - `for` as text until doors exist (cl.b014.s07:f0.d1).
   I'll also write the implied spots, so you don't derive them twice: a bench spot per bench prop (cap from its piece's size), bus-stop waits from bus-stop shelters, and café seats when terraces exist. Each carries an IMPLIED flag.

5. Obstacles (12.3 #5). The blocks' props already carry type, yaw and scale. So your per-PropType sizes apply to them as to today's props, and I add no section.
   If you want the catalog's own colliders instead (collide= box or cylinder, at size= times scale), say so and I'll add "blocks.colliders": a box or cylinder per prop, in world space.

Order on my side, once you agree: parcels and lots first (curb cuts need them), then walk, then spots, each a sign-off with tests and a round trip. The pilot's ~25 block files (2c) then fill them.
