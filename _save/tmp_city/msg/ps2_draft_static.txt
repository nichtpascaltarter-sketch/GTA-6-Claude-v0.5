2. THE CATALOG
- lib.veg.shrub: size=1.80x1.80x1.60 becomes 6.40x6.40x2.90. That's twice 3.162 m (6.32) and 2.898 m, each rounded up to 0.1 m.
- The comment above the pieces now says a shrub's size is its variants' largest: its foliage's reach, across any way it turns. A tree's height stays the average, as its h= needs.
- Nothing reads it in a way that changes the compile:
  - compileBlocks scales a plant by its scale= alone; size.z serves only a tree's h=.
  - The colliders take size= only for collide=box or cylinder, and the shrub is collide=none.
  - worldv reads no piece's size.
- Shown: S's world with only the new catalog compiles to S's world.bin except the manifest (10,880 to 10,882 B, hash fea15417e538bd7c to af48e9f43b1beef8). The other 16 sections are byte for byte the same.
- One copy keeps the old size: tests/world/worldmodel_test.cpp has its own inline catalog with the shrub at 1.80x1.80x1.60. It tests the parser, not the world, so I left it.

3. dress.py
- SHRUB_W now comes from the catalog (6.4), so a shrub reaches 3.2 m times its scale.
- It shrinks to fit as before, but never below scale 0.25 (a reach of 0.8 m); a shrub that would need less is dropped.
- The rule order (front yard, nearer side yard, farther side yard, drop) and the 1.2 m ways are as they were.
- The true size exposed three things, and I fixed them:
  - The clear width across a side yard is now measured over the shrub's own reach along the yard, which shrinks with it. S measured it over the full-size reach; at 3 m, that window ran into the street and next door's ways, and threw out yards a small shrub fits. The scan down the yard now starts at the least reach too.
  - A shrub now keeps clear of every footprint and every building's ways in its block, not only its own building's. With the true size but only its own building's ways, a first dry run left 9 shrubs reaching next door's ways (b008.h16's, for one).
  - A 0.05 m margin is kept to walls, ways and the lot's edge, and the scale is floored to 0.01 rather than rounded, so rounding never takes a canopy over its line.
- A shrub on a lot without a building (there are none today) would keep clear the same way, or be dropped.
