HOW
- The sections follow the road overrides' conventions: RoadNetwork edge indices, a distance along an edge in the RoadEdge's own s, sides looking fwd (0 left, 1 right), world xyz with z on the graded ground, lists in block-file order. Each is read like roadsbin: all of it or nothing, and a damaged section is refused. The format is in blocksbin.h's header.
- "blocks.parcels": per parcel its id, use, the edge and side it fronts (or none) and its grade (NaN where it follows the terrain). Curb cuts and buildings will name a parcel by its index.
- "blocks.lots": per lot its id, levels, entry and exit cuts ({edge, s, side}; no exit where the entry serves both) and rows (bay line, bay count, width, length, facing, level, the aisle link), then its drive graph: nodes (xyz, level, kind: aisle, entry, exit, ramp), links (a and b, width, one-way a to b, kind: aisle, drive, ramp, polyline xyz) and bays (centre xyz, facing, row, the link it opens onto, and s, plan metres along that link).
- worldc builds the graph (worldmodel.h compileBlockSections):
  - Bays are spread evenly along each row's line and face its yaw= (without one, square to the line, to its left).
  - The aisle is behind the bays, half their depth square to the line plus half its width away: 6 m, or 4 m and one way where the bays are more than 15 degrees off square, running the way that turns a car into them by less than 90 degrees. A row's aisle= overrides it, e.g. a loop round an island.
  - Rows that face each other share one aisle down the middle: straight aisles within 1.5 m, parallel, overlapping by a metre and running the same way, or polylines whose points are all within 1.5 m.
  - Aisles become links with a node at each end; ends within 1 m share one node, and a closed loop gets two links. A ramp is a link from its foot to its top, a level each, 3 m apart (kLotLevelHeight). An aisle or ramp end within 1 m of an aisle's side joins it there.
  - The entry and exit cuts stand behind their side's sidewalk and drive to the nearest point of an aisle on level 0, splitting it there: one way in and one way out where the lot has both.
  - Any part the entry can't reach joins it by its nearest pair of nodes on one level. Then, along the one-way links, a node that can't reach the way out joins the nearest node on its level that can, and one the entry doesn't reach joins the nearest it does (an angled row's far end, say). So every node lies on a way from the entry to the way out.
  - Each bay opens onto its row's aisle where that passes nearest its centre.
  - Heights: each point stands on the graded ground plus its level's lift (placeOnTerrain), the same as props.
- Findings: block.lot (levels under 1, no rows, a row of no bays, a row or ramp on a level the lot lacks, a cut past its edge's ends, nothing on level 0 for the entry, a level no ramp reaches) and block.shape (lines too short). A lot with a finding is left out.
