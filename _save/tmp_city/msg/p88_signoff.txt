WHAT (player-facing, in hand and procedural mode: they share the streaming)
- A frame uploads at most 64k triangles of streamed cells. A cell that would take it past that waits for the next frame, and one bigger than 64k still goes, alone, in a frame of its own.
- Cells now upload near before far, nearest first. One thing you should know: 87 didn't do this. It took ready cells in its hash map's order, so this order is new.
- What that changes:
  - Hand drive: the t=0.10 frame no longer carries 45,41 and 46,41 together. 46,41 goes alone (62.4k triangles, 6.9 ms), 47,41 with 45,40 the next frame, and 45,41 the frame after. That frame dropped from 33.2 ms to 24.0 ms.
  - Procedural drive: the start used to upload 4 near cells per frame (227-260k triangles, 26-30 ms). Now each goes alone, in 7.9-10.6 ms.
  - A near cell no longer waits behind far ones. In 87's hand drive, 45,40 near (11.5k triangles) sat ready for 43 frames (1.4 s) while far cells ahead of it in the map's order went first. That's the late near cell your (a) rule watches for. In 88, no cell with triangles waits more than 4 frames there.
  - The cost: the procedural drive's near ring completes 11 frames later (frame 17, not 6), one 32-90k cell a frame. Far cells queue behind it: their mean wait at the start went from 14 frames to 25. That's invisible here, where the first frame takes 15 s.

HOW
- WorldRenderer::update collects the generated cells and sorts them: near before far, then by the camera's distance to the cell's square (the rings' measure), then by key. It uploads in that order until it has done 4 cells (as before) or the next would pass uploadTriangles = 64000. The frame's first cell always goes.

The hand drive's 15.5 ms streaming max, and where the streaming time goes. I timed the update by parts with a third scratch exe:
- That frame (t=0.27) uploaded only 7k triangles of far cells.
- On a quiet run, a big cell's upload takes 5.4-6.4 ms in the hand drive and 5.1-7.4 ms in the procedural one (two outliers there, at 10.3 and 13.2 ms). Creating the GPU buffers is about 1 ms of that (0.7-2.2). The other ~5 ms is upload()'s CPU side: the copy into a temporary array, the lamp arms and storefront lights, adding the collision to the physics world, and freeing the geometry.
- The spikes without uploads come from the request loop, not the uploads: 3.4-11.3 ms in frames 8-13, while the far ring is being asked for. The loop does almost no work, so that's waiting, most likely for a core or the job queue's lock, while the three workers and the software renderer's threads fill the four cores.
- The request code is 87's, and 86's and 87's runs show the same kind of spike (6.6-8.6 ms in frames that uploaded only empty cells).
- So the budget holds the uploads to one big cell a frame: 5-7 ms here, with the 13.2 ms outlier on a lone 87.5k procedural cell. The streaming max isn't under 10 ms in every run, though.
