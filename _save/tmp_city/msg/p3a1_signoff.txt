WHAT (player-facing)

HOW
- The world model types what the shells need (worldmodel.h):
  - a plan's class, size, footprint, floors (layout, z, h), roof (form, pitch, overhang, material), facade style and palette, and its options;
  - a layout's walls, doors, windows and rooms;
  - a facade style's wall, trims, window, door and base;
  - a palette's colours;
  - a building's place and config: palette, roof, facade, and its option choices.
- compileBuildings (worldmodel.h) makes:
  - a look per facade style: the wall's material and palette slot, the trims, the window's kind, frame and shutters, the door's colour, and the base. A material is a catalog piece whose code= names a MaterialId (MAT_STUCCO). Without them: stucco walls, a painted metal frame, and trims in the wall's material, 0.2 m high.
  - a shell per plan: class, size, footprint, look, palette (packed as the renderer packs colours), roof, floors, and each floor's exterior walls (ext=) with their doors and windows. A roof without a material is gravel when flat and shingles when pitched.
    - Each window carries the light schedule of the room behind it: the room's sched= where that names a roomlight.h class, else its type's. Living, dining and hall rooms get home_living; kitchen, bed and bath their home classes; shop, office, restaurant and garage their own; bar gets nightlife; stair, corridor and lobby always; store, plant and utility never.
  - a placement per building file: its shell, at= (the plan's origin), z=, yaw (in radians), mirror=, and its config's palette, roof material and look in place of the plan's.
  - Findings:
    - building.kind: a material whose code the game lacks;
    - building.shape: a plan without a floor, or without a footprint of 3 points;
    - building.option: a config field that names no option of the plan, or a choice the option lacks. The format said worldc checks these, but nothing did.
- buildingsbin.h has "library" (the looks and shells) and "buildings" (the placements). Both are LZ-compressed, versioned and little-endian, and each is written only when it has something.
  - readBuildings reads all of it or nothing. It refuses a section that is damaged, truncated, over-long or of another version. It also refuses an enum past its range, a colour over 24 bits, a window schedule past roomlight.h's, a wall on a floor the shell lacks, and a building naming a shell or look the library lacks.
  - Each list's read bound is its record's least size (44, 16, 68, 12, 32, 24 and 56 bytes).
- Names: the enum types are Hand* (HandDoorKind, HandRoofForm and the rest), because World::DoorKind (interiors.h) and World::RoofForm (buildings.h) already exist. The first MinGW check caught that. Their constants (PAL_, TRIM_, WIN_, DOOR_, SHUTTER_, RFORM_) clash with nothing in src or the MinGW headers.
- Docs: NTW_FORMAT section 1, and a list of what the buildings compiler reads after 9.10; HANDBUILT_WORLD 6.1.
- Tests (worldmodel_test testBuildings), on a shop with a flat over it and a cottage on the flat's floor plate, with a facade style, two palettes and two buildings:
  - the look: its trims (the none trim left out) and the defaults;
  - the shells: class, palette packing, roof defaults, floors, the 8 exterior walls without the partitions, the openings in layout order, and each window's schedule, from its room's type or from sched=;
  - the placements: yaw, mirror, the config's overrides and an option's choice;
  - the round trip, exact, and no sections at all without buildings;
  - 9 damaged cases refused: both sections truncated, a byte over, both versions, a colour slot, a schedule, and a shell and a look past the library's. Placements without the library are refused too.
  - 100 of the smallest records of each list read back. I checked each read bound by mutation: one byte over its record's least fails its own case.
  - the findings: 2 building.kind, 1 building.shape and 2 building.option.

NEXT: 3a-2, under way. One question on it, for the night lighting:
- The facade shader draws windows on a regular grid. It picks each room's schedule class itself (facadeRoomSchedule) from the room's hash and the building's flags. So it cannot take a hand window's class as it stands, contrary to my plan's hope. Two ways:
  (a) No shader change. Each window is a glass quad whose facade uv maps into one window cell of a per-look entry. Each room gets the cell whose hash makes the shader's own rules pick its class, found on the CPU with a C++ copy of the shader's hash. That reaches the home classes, office, always, shop and restaurant; nightlife falls back to restaurant and garage to never. It breaks silently if facadeRoomSchedule changes, unless a test vector pins it.
  (b) About 4 lines in facade.hlsli: a flag bit 64, "hand-built window", whose class comes in flags bits 8-11 and is used in place of facadeRoomSchedule. Every class is reachable, with no hash search. Procedural facades never set the bit, so they render as before.
- I'd take (b). Until you answer, I'm writing the mesher and the content, which are the same either way.
