CHANGELOG (player-facing)
- Lateral orbital rims: each eye socket is now closed at the side, where the cheekbone rises to the tail of the brow. Before, the socket left a trench 6-10 mm deep at eye level that ran past the outer corner of the eye to the temple. Its upper wall faced steeply down, so under a lamp or a high sun the socket's shade ran on across the side of the face. Now the skin beside the eye runs smoothly from the brow's tail down to the cheekbone, and the dip is at most 2.5 mm.
- No more pale crescent under each eye. The skin edge of the lower lid had taken the normal of the lid margin, which faces straight up (+68 deg). That lit the top of the lower lid as a white "smile" under the iris. The edge is now rounded (+24 deg), so only the 1 mm margin itself catches the light.
- Honest size of the effect: it shows best at 0.5 m by day (the lower lids), and in three-quarter views under a lamp (the dark patch beside the outer corner of the eye is gone). Straight on at 1 m and 4 m the change is subtle. The night "goggles" at 4 m remain: see the renderer note below.

RENDERER NOTES (for you to pass on; nothing here is in my files)
1. Night sockets. At 1 m under the viewer's lamp, Mari's eye sockets get about 1.4% of her forehead's light (linear). They are nearly black, and at 4 m every face reads as wearing dark glasses. Local lights have no bounce: a lamp lights the pavement, and nothing comes back up onto the undersides of the face. I'm testing a small, self-contained fix in a private --shaderdir override that is never shipped: a ground bounce of ~12% for SM_SKIN and SM_EYE, 6 lines in lighting.hlsl's local-light loop. I'll send it as a proposal with before/after shots once the runs are done.
2. A 1-px pale line above dark brows at 1 m. It is not in the lighting: debug views of the sun's diffuse, ambient and specular terms show no line there. It is the tonemap's sharpening (post.cpp p3.z = 0.35) putting a halo next to the dark brow. This is part of the "pale outline" look around brows and lash lines at 1 m.
