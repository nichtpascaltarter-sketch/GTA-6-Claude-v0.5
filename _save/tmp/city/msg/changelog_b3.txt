- Motels in four real types instead of one box: fifties MiMo motor courts (eave slab, steel walkway with coloured panels, boomerang pylon sign), Mediterranean motels (barrel-tile roofs, stucco piers and parapet, blade sign), Keys motels in wood and tin (two-level wooden gallery, painted sign) and seventies motor inns (shingled mansard, pole sign), with lobby ends, back wings and sign towers. Key Solano's side streets no longer look stamped.
- Strip malls in three types: mission-revival strips (tile-roofed arcade on stucco piers, entry tower), MiMo strips (folded-plate canopy on pipe columns, pylon sign at the street) and power centres (tall parapet, anchor end, metal canopy). Canopy supports stand on the stall lines, clear of the parked cars.
- Farms in Redland and the farmlands: Florida cracker farmhouses (raised, deep porch with steps, tin roof), two-storey I-houses and ranch farmhouses in white, cream and weathered board; gambrel barns with X-braced doors and hay doors, open pole barns, and steep gable barns with lean-to sheds.
- Neighbourhood churches on the boulevards and in the small towns in four types: mission revival (stepped espadana gable with a bell, tile roof, corner bell tower), white clapboard with a steeple, brick Gothic with a pinnacled corner tower, and sixties A-frames with a glazed front and a free-standing bell pylon. The church envelope and front are unchanged, so the bride encounter's chapel stop is unaffected.
- Far LOD is lighter: porches, columns, rails, posts, stairs and folded plates are drawn only up close. Far-ring triangles for houses are back near the old cost (whole-map far LOD 1.22 M after batch 2, 1.01 M now; 0.60 M before batch 1).
