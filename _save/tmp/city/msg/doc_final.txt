SUMMARY (the document has the detail and the reasons)
- Terrain: bake it once from today's generator into data. Keep the natural terrain and the port island's quay walls, ring and berth. Drop the pads and the road grading; the world compiler redoes them under authored roads and parcels. The prototype confirmed the terrain separates: the sites and roads edit only 5.9 km² (3.2% of the land), and the baked heights (int16 cm, row-delta coded) compress to ~3.5 MB.
- Format: "NTW" text, one record per line, blocks closed by `end`, kept canonical by a formatter. world/ is laid out as districts/<d>/blocks/<b>/<building>.ntw, one file per building (~35.6k), plus lib/ with one item per file, so each agent owns whole files.
- Buildings: each placement is a library plan, plus its configuration (mirror, palette, roof, options, units, fit-out and sign, furnishing variant), plus overrides. Windows belong to the floor layouts' exterior walls and the facade is built from them, so a window always matches the room behind it.
- Library, at today's density of 35,617 buildings: ~600 plans, 270 floor layouts, 155 units, 165 fit-outs, 200 furniture sets and 250 new kit pieces, about 1,600 items. Houses alone get ~92k exterior combinations. A "no visible twin" check per block face, contact sheets and street views guard against repetition.
- Build and ship: `worldc` (a host tool, run from build.sh and build.bat) compiles the text to world.bin: ~70 MB, or 20-30 MB with our own LZ codec. It bakes the navmeshes; the game rebuilds meshes and collision at load and caches them. Recommendation: embed world.bin in the exe as an RCDATA resource (exe ~35-45 MB), so the exe-only autoupdater keeps working unchanged. A data pack would need a manifest, verification of both files and an atomic swap, and is only worth it above ~200 MB.
- Runtime: `--world=hand|procedural`, with procedural as the default. In hand mode generateCell instantiates compiled data and invents nothing. Exteriors use three levels: close detail within 100 m (instanced), a near shell within 420 m (1,000-1,500 triangles per house, merged per cell) and far massing. Interiors that aren't loaded show through interior-mapped windows, chosen from the real room behind each window.
- Interiors at scale: loaded per building and per floor, on approach (35 m), on door use (the door animation covers the load), on the stairs and in the lift; dropped behind. Budgets: at most 24 buildings, 48 floors, 384 MB GPU and 96 MB CPU; 30 ms per floor on a layout's first use, 3 ms cached. An 85-floor tower keeps only the lobby, the player's floor ±1 and the lift's destination resident. Collision and navmesh are per layout, so every floor that uses a layout shares them.
- NPCs inside: a pure occupancy function per building, unit, hour and day. People exist only on loaded floors, with deterministic identities. What changes folds back into a small per-building delta in the save. Unloaded buildings cost nothing.
- Validator: a full run over all 35k buildings in under a minute, incremental in under 5 s. It checks that every room is reachable, stairs and lifts, overlaps, windows against rooms, roads and signals, dressing and places. Previews: maps, floor plans, cutaways, contact sheets, street views. Also `--only=<building>` and `--viewer layout:/plan:`.
- Migration: stage 0 foundations (2-3 weeks). Then a pilot of the heart of Calle Luna (439 buildings, 834 floors, Act 1's Calle Luna missions, ~800 agent-hours). Then the core, the rest of the city, rural, the switch, and finally removal of the generator, only after a release in hand mode and with the user's agreement. No hybrid mode: hand mode shows only the districts built so far. Missions re-anchor through places.ntw, which fills the same Place structs.
- Effort: ~6,700 agent-hours ±40% (library 1,850, content 2,820, engineering 1,000, missions 150, contingency 870). That is ~105 working days with 8 agents, or ~70 with 12. One owner per file; the pilot recalibrates the estimate.
- Open decisions D1-D7 (section 14) are for you and the user: embedding vs a pack, ground cover, the tracing underlay, the pilot, interior-mapped windows, no hybrid mode, a precompiled lane graph.
