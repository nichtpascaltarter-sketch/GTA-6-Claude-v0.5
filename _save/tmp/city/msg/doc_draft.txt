SUMMARY (the document has the detail and the reasons)
- Terrain: bake it once from today's generator into data. Keep the natural terrain plus the port island's quay walls, ring and berth; drop the pads and the road grading, which the world compiler redoes under authored roads and parcels. The prototype ran it: the terrain separates cleanly. The sites and roads edit only 5.9 km² (3.2% of the land), and the baked heights (int16 cm, row-delta) come to ~3.5 MB compressed.
- Format: "NTW" text, one record per line, blocks end with `end`, a formatter keeps it canonical. world/ is laid out as districts/<d>/blocks/<b>/<building>.ntw (one file per building, ~35.6k) plus lib/ (one item per file), so each agent owns whole files.
- Buildings: a placement is a library plan + configuration (mirror, palette, roof, options, units, fit-out and sign, furnishing variant) + overrides. Windows belong to the floor layouts' exterior walls, and the facade is built from them, so a window always matches the room behind it.
- Library: ~600 plans, 270 floor layouts, 155 units, 165 fit-outs, 200 furniture sets and 250 new kit pieces, about 1,600 items. Houses alone get ~92k exterior combinations. Repetition is checked by a "no visible twin" rule per block face, and by contact sheets and street views.
- Build and ship: `worldc` (host tool in build.sh/build.bat) compiles text to world.bin: ~60 MB, or 20-30 MB with our own LZ codec. I recommend embedding it in the exe as an RCDATA resource (exe ~35-45 MB). The exe-only autoupdater then works unchanged. A data pack would need a manifest, two-file verification and an atomic swap, and is only worth it above ~200 MB.
- Runtime: `--world=hand|procedural`, procedural stays the default. In hand mode, generateCell instantiates compiled data and invents nothing. Exteriors come in three levels: close detail within 100 m (instanced), a near shell within 420 m (1,000-1,500 triangles per house, merged per cell) and far massing. Unloaded interiors show through interior-mapped windows picked from the real room records.
- Interiors at scale: loaded per building and per floor, on approach (35 m), on door use (the door animation covers the load), on the stairs and in the lift. Dropped behind. Budgets: at most 24 buildings, 48 floors, 384 MB GPU and 96 MB CPU; at most 30 ms per floor on a layout's first use and 3 ms cached. An 85-floor tower keeps only the lobby, the player's floor ±1 and the lift's destination resident. Collision and navmesh are compiled per layout and shared by every floor that uses it.
- NPCs inside: a pure occupancy function per building, unit, hour and day. People are materialised only on loaded floors with deterministic identities, and changes fold back into a small per-building delta in the save. Unloaded buildings cost nothing.
- Validator: all 35k buildings in under a minute, incremental in under 5 s. It checks reachability of every room, stairs and lifts, overlaps, windows against rooms, roads and signals, dressing and places. Previews: maps, floor plans, cutaways, contact sheets, street views. Plus `--only=<building>` and `--viewer layout:/plan:`.
- Migration: stage 0 foundations (2-3 weeks), then a pilot of the heart of Calle Luna: 439 buildings, 834 floors, Act 1's Calle Luna missions, ~800 agent-hours. Then the core, the rest and rural, the switch, and removal of the generator only after a release in hand mode and the user's agreement. No hybrid mode: hand mode shows only the districts built so far. Missions re-anchor through places.ntw, filling the same Place structs.
- Effort: ~6,700 agent-hours ±40% (library 1,850, content 2,820, engineering 1,000, missions 150, contingency 870). That is ~105 working days with 8 agents or ~70 with 12. One owner per file, and the pilot recalibrates.
- Open decisions D1-D7 (section 14) are for you and the user: embed vs pack, ground cover, the tracing underlay, the pilot, interior-mapped windows, no hybrid mode, a precompiled lane graph.
