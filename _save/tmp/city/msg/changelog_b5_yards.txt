- Back-yard pools show. Before, the water sat under the lawn and the ground, so from the air every pool read as a square of grass inside its deck. Now the deck stands on the yard's highest corner with a skirt down to the ground, and the lawn is cut away under it. Pools are glossy turquoise, and most are lit from below at night. They come as rectangles, with rounded ends, or (behind villas) as lap pools, some with a spa on one end. Decks are brick pavers, keystone, poured concrete or, in the Keys, wood. Pools stay in the far LOD as plain water, so the suburbs keep them from the air.
- Front yards by district, with gaps for the driveway and the front walk:
  - Calle Luna, the Flats, North Porto Sol and Midtown: stucco knee walls under steel rejas between capped pillars (black, white, green or bronze), low garden walls and black aluminium fences;
  - Westbrook: mostly open lawns, some with garden walls, rejas, aluminium fences, coral rock or pickets;
  - the Grove and the islands: coral-rock walls;
  - the Keys, Key Solano and Ten Palms: white picket fences;
  - the small towns: pickets, ranch rails and aluminium fences;
  - the country: ranch rails.
  Picket fences also stand before many Victorians and conch houses, and rejas before Mediterranean and block houses. Villas in the Grove and on the islands get coral-rock walls or tall rejas instead of the one stucco wall.
- Back-yard fences where a house has no hedges: wood board fences, painted block walls or white vinyl, by district. They run along the back lot line and one side, with returns to the house behind the garage and a gate on the other side.
- Driveways by district: poured concrete, brick pavers (the suburbs, Mediterranean houses, villas, the Grove), old asphalt (the older city and the towns), crushed shell and gravel (the Keys and the country).
- Front walks from the stoop to the street: concrete, brick pavers (Mediterranean houses and villas) or stepping stones (the Keys and the Grove). A yard tree on the walk moves aside.
- Mailboxes: the wooden post, a stucco pillar with a slot (the suburbs, the villas, and wherever a garden wall runs along the front), or a black box on a steel post in the country.
- The lawn no longer pokes through driveways and carport floors on uneven ground.
- Paved front yards in the older city: about a quarter of the houses in Calle Luna and the Flats, a sixth in North Porto Sol and Midtown, and a few in Westbrook have concrete or brick-paver front yards. The driveway is part of the paving, often behind a reja.
- Block houses keep their gardens. Since batch 4 a third of them had lost their lawn, hedges and mailbox, because the window awnings used up the house's detail budget. The garden now has a budget of its own.
- Scattered trees keep off strip-mall and gas-station forecourts, driveways, pool decks and paved front yards. On main, a palm grows up through one gas station's tile canopy.
