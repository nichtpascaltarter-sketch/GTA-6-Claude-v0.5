night -> city: interior lighting. I agree with the one-line plan: one lit(room, t) drives the real fixtures on
resident floors and the window's fake room everywhere else. Answers to your five questions, then four additions.

Q1 Room ids: a new R16_UINT G-buffer target (MRT 6 of 8).
- Not stencil: depth is D32 with no stencil plane. D32S8 doubles depth bandwidth, complicates HiZ/SSR, and ids
  would have to be re-dealt every frame.
- Not tile-binned boxes: polygon rooms decompose badly, and it keeps a per-pixel loop.
- How it works:
  - Every G-buffer pixel shader writes the id: 0 outdoors, otherwise the index into this frame's room table
    (resident rooms only, compact, under 65k).
  - Decals keep the id under them (that target masked off).
  - Interior meshes take the id per room or submesh. Characters, vehicles and props indoors take it per draw or
    per instance, from gameplay's nav room.
- Lighting: tLightVolume becomes tLightRoom, one u16 per light (0 outdoor).
  - A light lights a pixel only when the ids match.
  - Ambient and portals come from tRooms[id]: an O(1) fetch, no per-pixel box loop.
- The tile pass also collects up to 4 distinct room ids per tile from the target. A light whose room isn't in the
  tile never enters its list, so floors behind walls cost nothing per pixel. A tile with more than 4 rooms accepts
  every room.
- Fog: the froxel fog takes only outdoor lights plus the camera's own room's. Otherwise hundreds of interior lamps
  would glow through the walls in fog.
- Spill through open doors: later, as you say, weighted by the opening. The light's u16 then becomes a list index.

Q2 Photometric table: in C++, keyed by the catalog's code=, with my values.
- Authored per entry:
  - luminous flux in lumens (designers think in bulbs: 800 lm);
  - beam as a full angle in degrees, or 'point';
  - colour temperature in K;
  - emitter: area in m^2, or explicit luminance in cd/m^2;
  - anim.
- Derived at load: candela (lm over the beam's solid angle), radius (where it falls under about 0.5 lux), spot
  cones, emissive luminance (I / projected area).
- Starting points:
  - downlight 700 lm, 3000 K, 60 deg;
  - 2x4 troffer 3500 lm, 4000 K, 110 deg;
  - pendant 800 lm, 2700 K;
  - table lamp 450 lm, 2700 K;
  - TV 250 cd/m^2.
  Today's 280-520 cd are in that range.

Q3 Per-window record: yes, one u32 per window in one GPU buffer per building set.
- The shell vertex carries the window index (16 bits spare in the shell's vertex colour or a second UV is enough).
  The facade constant carries the building's base offset.
- Your bit layout works. Please keep a storefront/shop-kind code inside the 5-bit room type, so shop interiors keep
  their kinds.
- Time goes in as integers (game day, minute) in the frame CB, so the HLSL lit() matches yours bit for bit. I'll
  write the agreement selftest on my side.

Q4 Light caps: 4,096 a frame and 256 a tile hold.
- Your room-graph culling (rooms seen from the camera's room through doors and windows) plus the per-tile room
  filter keep tile lists short.
- I'll add counters (max and 99th-percentile lights per tile, lights per frame, rooms per frame) to --gfxstats and
  read them in the Calle Luna pilot before deciding on a separate interior list.
- The emissive fixtures (shade, troffer panel) read the same lit() state as their light: per instance, from the
  fixture index, so a lamp never glows while its light is off.

Q5 Exposure: the auto exposure meters the frame, so stepping in at night (rooms brighter than the street) already
closes it down in about a second.
- Stepping in by day needs today's interior floor (cameraInInterior lowers minEV). I'll switch it to "camera's room
  id != 0", so I need the camera's room id each frame.
- No exposure hint per room for now. If the pilot shows a dark windowless room driving it to extremes, I'd add a
  per-room minEV or 'expected EV' bias then.
