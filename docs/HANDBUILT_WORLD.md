# The hand-built world: architecture proposal

Status: v1. The lead approved decisions D1-D7 on 2026-10-19 (section 14), and section 12 holds the AI agent's
requirements. The user's word overrides any of it.
Author: the city agent. Date: 2026-10-19.
Scope: replace the procedural world of Palmera with a hand-authored one, keeping only the terrain.

The numbers in this document come from today's code and from three small prototypes run for it: a terrain bake, a
building census and a cell census (section 13). Effort figures are planning estimates. The pilot district (section 10)
calibrates them.

---

## 0. Summary

**What stays:** the terrain (ground shape, coast, rivers, canals, the lake, the sea), baked once from today's generator
into a data file. The engine, the renderer, gameplay, AI, story and audio keep their code. They read the new world through
the same in-memory structures they read today, wherever that is possible.

**What is new:** everything on the terrain. Roads, parcels, all 35,617 buildings, landmarks, vegetation, props and lights
are authored as text by agents, compiled to a binary world and embedded in the exe. Every building is enterable, on every
floor and in every room.

**How 35k enterable buildings stay hand-made:**
- **A library of hand-designed parts.** About 1,600 items in all: building plans, floor layouts, apartment, office and shop
  units, fit-outs and furniture sets.
- **Each building placed and configured by hand.** Every placement chooses a plan, mirror, palette, options, unit
  assignments and a furniture variant, with its own overrides where they matter.
- **Static data.** Placement scripts may help an agent lay out a street. Their output is reviewed text, and the game never
  generates anything at runtime.

**Data:** about 70 MB of text in the repo, compiled to about 20-30 MB compressed. That is embedded in `NeonTide.exe` as a
resource, so the exe-only autoupdater keeps working (section 6).

**Runtime:**
- **Exteriors** stream with today's 256 m cells and LOD rings, still one draw per cell.
- **Interiors** load per building and per floor near the player, or when a door is used, and are dropped behind.
- **Collision and nav** belong to each floor layout and are shared by every building that uses it. The compiler bakes
  the navmeshes.
- **Indoor NPCs** come from a cheap per-building occupancy model. They are spawned only into loaded floors (section 7.6).

**Migration:** a `--world=hand|procedural` switch, with procedural staying the default until the hand world is playable.
The first build is a pilot: the heart of Calle Luna (439 buildings), end to end with Act 1's Calle Luna missions. Then
the city, district by district. The
generator is deleted only after the hand world passes the story regression.

**Effort:** about 6,700 agent-hours (±40%). That is about 105 working days with 8 agents, which this machine sustains
if content work runs on host tools, or about 70 with 12 agents on two machines (section 11).

## 1. Goals and constraints

**Content:**
- Keep the map: 20.48 km square, with land spanning 15.8 × 19.5 km of it, 183.5 km² of land and 235.9 km² of water
  (section 13.1). Keep only the terrain.
- Keep today's density: 35,617 buildings, split as houses 22,192, shops 4,600, midrise 2,411, warehouses 1,620, villas
  901, deco 688, strip malls 640, condos 427 and towers 352. The remaining 1,764 are factories, farmhouses, barns,
  motels, gas stations, churches and shacks.
- The generator also makes 3,480 back garages and sheds on house and shop lots. The hand world keeps them as small
  outbuilding plans placed with their lot.
- The census (section 13.2) gives the volume behind those numbers:
  - **79,200 floors and 47.2 km² of floor space**, of which towers hold 18.9 km² (40%);
  - 81 towers have 50 floors or more, and the tallest has 85;
  - at ~20 m² a room, that is **about 2.4 million rooms**, every one of them enterable.
- Every floor and every room of every building is enterable.
- "Hand-made" means designed and placed deliberately:
  - Nothing is generated by random rules at runtime.
  - Reusing hand-designed pieces is expected, as in any hand-built game: a furniture set, an apartment unit on many floors,
    a house plan along a street.
  - Deterministic compilation of authored data is allowed: extruding walls from a floor plan, grading the ground under an
    authored road, merging meshes per cell.

**Engineering:**
- No third-party code, so the parser, the compressor and the tools are ours (README).
- MSVC and MinGW builds, and the MSVC-safety rules.
- The game ships as one exe, and the autoupdater replaces only the exe.

**Process:**
- Text that agents write and review.
- Diff-friendly.
- Laid out so that many agents work in parallel without touching the same files.

## 2. Today's pipeline, and the seam we cut along

Startup builds the world in this order. The timings are from a regression log: native CPU under wine, on a busy machine.

| Step | Code | Time | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terrain | `WorldMap::generate` (worldmap.cpp) | 13 s with the sites | 2560² heights (8 m), water levels, regions, splat; coast, channels, lake |
| Special sites | `SiteSet::layout` (sites.cpp, places.cpp), called inside `generate` | (inside) | airport, port, landmarks, places. Also: terrain landforms (port island, basins), pads, reserved areas |
| Transit layout | `transitLayout` (transit.cpp), called from the sites | (inside) | SkyLine corridor and stations, ferry piers |
| Roads | `RoadNetwork::generate` (roads.cpp) | 4.9 s | 6,223 nodes, 9,547 edges, 1,690 signalised; 61.7 km of highway and 1,165.8 km of streets. Also grades the terrain |
| Buildings | `BuildingSet::generate` (buildings.cpp, blockstyle.cpp) | 5.6 s | 35,617 envelopes with style and archetype, open lots, facade records |
| Interiors | `planInteriors` (interiors.cpp) | 6 ms | 29 enterable interiors (one per host building, ground floor) |
| Game init | lanes.cpp, population.cpp, mission_util.cpp, mapview.cpp | 3 s / – / 0.2 s / 24 s | lane graph (25,496 lanes, 56,760 connectors, 40,687 walk nodes); 3,955 venues; ~60 mission places; the 2560² map texture |

Cells are generated on demand:
- The renderer streams 256 m cells: near LOD within 420 m, far LOD within 2,300 m.
- Each streamed cell calls `World::generateCell(cx, cy, detail, CellGeometry&)` (cellgen.cpp) on a job thread.
- That call returns one opaque mesh, decals, prop instances, lights and collision boxes for the cell.
- Interiors are built on jobs when the camera comes within 480 m (`interiors_game.cpp`), because the facade cut-outs show
  them from the near ring.

**The seam.** Consumers reach the world through a small set of globals and calls:

| Consumer | Reads |
|---|---|
| Renderer (world_render.cpp) | `generateCell`, `siteFarCells`, facade records (`BuildingSet::facades`) |
| Physics | `CellGeometry::collision`, interior shell collision |
| Traffic / ped AI (lanes.cpp, pednav.cpp) | `gRoads` (nodes, edges, classes, lanes, sidewalks, signals), `gSites->walks`, `gBuildings` (doors and lots) |
| Police, events, population | `gBuildings`, `gSites`, `regionAt` |
| Missions (mission_util.cpp `computePlaces`) | ~60 places resolved from hint positions to nearby buildings and frontages |
| Transit (transit_game.cpp) | `gTransit` (SkyLine, bus routes and stops, ferries, the streetcar) |
| Map UI (mapview.cpp) | `gMap`, `gRoads`, `gBuildings`, `gSites`, built into textures at `hudInit` |
| Ambience (gameworld.cpp to audio) | region, urban density, coast distance around the listener |
| Interiors (interiors_game.cpp) | `gInteriors` (rooms, doors, portals, scenario points, markers) |

The plan keeps this seam:
- The hand world fills the same structures: a `RoadNetwork`, a building index, an interior set and a transit net.
- It implements `generateCell` from compiled data.
- Most consumers then run unchanged at first. Their richer authored inputs come later, where they matter: signal plans,
  per-floor nav, occupancy.

## 3. Terrain: separate it, bake it, grade it at compile time

### 3.1 What the terrain is today

`WorldMap::generate` builds, in one pass:
- the natural terrain: the coast from signed distance fields, hills, the Cypress Ridge, beaches, wetlands, Lake
  Okahatchee with its dike, and rivers and canals carved to sea level;
- then calls `SiteSet::layout`;
- then `recomputeSplat`.

Two systems reshape that natural terrain:

| Who | What | Keep in the bake? |
|---|---|---|
| Sites: landforms | Port Isle squared off to quay walls at yard level, with a dredged ring and a deep berth (sites.cpp `layoutPort`); the Key Coral golf pond (`carveBasin`) | **Yes.** They are coast and water depth: the quay edge, the berth and the channel round the island |
| Sites: pads | `flattenRect` / `padAA` under the airport, places, hospitals, the campus, the cemetery, the prison and the speedway | **No.** Those lots are re-authored, with their own grading |
| Roads | `flattenAlong` / `lowerAlong` under every edge and junction; `recomputeSplat` | **No.** The hand roads grade their own ground |

The terrain-bake prototype (section 13.1) measured each step:
- the sites change heights on 3.73 km², and turn 0.017 km² of land into water and 0.016 km² of water into land;
- the roads then grade 2.12 km² more. They turn no land into water, but they turn 0.041 km² of water into land: where a
  road runs less than 1 m above the water, `flattenAlong` fills the ground under it.

So the road grading separates cleanly. The site landforms and pads are interleaved in `SiteSet::layout`, and need the bake
switch below.

### 3.2 The bake

`tools/terrainbake.cpp` is a host tool, like `tools/worldcheck.cpp`, and has run once (stage 0, step 1):
1. It runs `WorldMap::generate` with the bake switch `gTerrainBakeLandforms` (sites.cpp). The switch makes
   `SiteSet::layout` run only the layouts that are landforms, the port island's quay walls, ring and berth and the golf
   pond's `carveBasin`, and drop what they place. `flattenRect`, `padAA` and every other site are skipped.
2. It doesn't run `RoadNetwork::generate`.
3. It writes `world/terrain/` as NTG grids, our lossless grid codec (`src/world/gridcodec.h`: a median predictor,
   adaptive Golomb-Rice residuals and runs), and checks that every file decodes to the same grid.

| File | Content | Raw | NTG |
|---|---|---|---|
| `height.ntg` | 2560² heights in cm, int16 (-44.93..166.85 m) | 13.1 MB | 2.30 MB |
| `water.ntg` | 2560² water class: 0 dry, 1 sea level, 2 lake level (levels in `terrain.ntw`) | 6.6 MB | 0.04 MB |
| `region.ntg` | 2560² today's district map | 6.6 MB | 0.04 MB |
| `coast.ntg` | 640² signed coast distance at 0.25 m (the beach-sand splat and ambience read it) | 0.8 MB | 0.28 MB |
| `terrain.ntw` | grid, layers and their hashes, water levels, coast, island and lake outlines, channels, the generator's commit | 31 KB | (text) |

The heights compress better than gzip -9 did on the same row-delta data in the prototype (3.4 MB).

After the bake, the terrain is data:
- The generator code is no longer needed for the hand world.
- Edits to the terrain are edits to these files, through a small brush tool or a re-bake. Both are rare and owned by one
  agent.

### 3.3 Grading and ground materials at compile time

The world compiler (section 6) grades the baked landform under authored roads and parcels. It uses the algorithms that
grade roads today (`flattenAlong`, `lowerAlong`, `flattenRect`, moved into the compiler):
- road profiles carry their own z;
- parcels carry a pad height and a blend;
- bridges and decks carry their own structure and don't grade.

Ground materials:
- The splat is recomputed at compile time from the graded heights (sand, rock, mud and grass by slope and height, as today).
- On top of that come authored paint layers per parcel: lawn, paving, gravel, dirt, sand, planting beds.
- The graded heights are stored in the compiled world, as a delta on the bake. The splat is recomputed at load from them
  and the paint layers, in about a second. The runtime never grades.

Ground cover (the grass blades and small decor that `grass.cpp` and `vegdecor.cpp` draw on the GPU) stays a shading effect
of the ground material, like a texture. It is not world content. Every tree, shrub, hedge and planter is placed by hand.
This is decision D2 (section 14).

## 4. Data model and text format

### 4.1 Principles

- **References plus overrides.** A placed building is a library plan, plus its configuration, plus a few overrides. The
  geometry lives once, in the library.
- **One owner per file.** Files are laid out by district, block and building. Library items are one file each.
- **Stable string IDs.** Missions, nav links, saves and overrides refer to IDs, never to indices or positions.
- **Static and explicit.** The text says exactly what is there. Scripts that help author write this text. The game reads
  it and never invents content.
- **Diff-friendly.** One record per line. Blocks end with `end`. A formatter keeps the field order and the number
  precision canonical, and the validator rejects unformatted files.

### 4.2 The text format ("NTW")

- UTF-8 with LF line ends, which `.gitattributes` keeps on every checkout (the `.ntg` grids are binary there). A `#`
  starts a comment. Blank lines are ignored.
- Every file starts with `ntw 1 <filekind> [key=value...]`.
- **Records.** A record is one line: `<type> [<id>] key=value key=value ...`.
- **Values:**
  - numbers in metres, degrees and seconds;
  - bare words;
  - "quoted strings";
  - lists `a,b,c`;
  - points `x,y` or `x,y,z`;
  - polylines and polygons `x,y;x,y;...`;
  - colours `#rrggbb` or palette names;
  - references to other IDs.
- **Blocks.** Some record types open a block that ends with a line `end`: a road `edge` (its lanes, crossings, stops,
  bays and kerb rules), a `junction` (its stop lines and connections), a `parcel` (its paint, fences and pads), a car
  park's `lot` (its bay rows and ramps) and a room's `furnish`. Indentation is for readers only: the parser uses `end`,
  so whitespace mistakes can't change the meaning.
- **Precision.** Positions use 2 decimals (cm), angles 1 decimal. The formatter enforces both.
- **IDs:**
  - lowercase `[a-z0-9_.-]`;
  - world entities are prefixed by their district (`cl.` for Calle Luna);
  - library items are prefixed by `lib.`;
  - inside a library item, element IDs are local (`w12`, `d3`, `bed1`).
- **Coordinates:**
  - World files use world metres (x east, y north, z up).
  - Library items use their own local frame. For plans and layouts that frame is: origin at the front-left corner of the
    footprint at ground-floor level, +x along the front, +y into the building. Those are today's interior axes, with the
    origin moved from the front centre to the front-left corner.

docs/NTW_FORMAT.md is the format's reference: the syntax, the conventions and every kind's records and fields, frozen
at v1. src/world/ntw.cpp is the parser and formatter, shared by the
compiler, the validator and the tools, and src/world/ntwschema.cpp holds the same schema in code.

- ~2,000-3,000 block files;

### 4.4 Roads

A road file holds nodes and edges. The compiler builds today's `RoadNetwork` from them (nodes, edges with centreline
points and z, class, lanes per direction, half width, sidewalks, flags, names). `LaneGraph::build` and everything
downstream then work unchanged. Lane-level detail is optional, and overrides what the lane graph derives where it is
written: turn pockets and lane use, each lane's moves, stop lines, signal plans, kerbside rules and stops. Section 12
gives the AI agent's requirement behind each record.

```
ntw 1 roads district=cl

node cl.n0013 at=1702.00,481.00 z=3.55 control=stop stops=cl.e0007,cl.e0009 # a two-way stop
edge cl.e0007 from=cl.n0012 to=cl.n0013 class=street name="SW 8th St" lanes=1/1 lanewidth=3.20 parking=both:2.40
  sidewalk=both:3.00 speed=13.4 shape=1702.00,365.50;1702.40,420.00;1702.00,481.00 profile=0.00:3.40;115.50:3.55
  lanes from=80.00 to=end dir=fwd count=2 add=left taper=12.00 # a left-turn pocket at the n0013 end
  noswitch dir=fwd from=95.00 to=end
  moves at=n1 lanes=l,sr
  crossing at=8.00 kind=signal # at the n0012 end, under the junction's walk signal
  crossing at=60.00 kind=zebra
  busstop cl.bus.08 at=40.00 side=right shelter=lib.prop.shelter_a wait=1704.80,403.00:3.00 routes=bus.8
  parkbay from=12.00 to=34.00 side=left kind=parallel meter=1
  nostop from=0.00 to=12.00 side=right why=junction
  loading from=44.00 to=56.00 side=left hours=6.00-11.00
  curbcut at=72.00 side=right w=4.00 to=cl.b014.lot1 # into the block's car park
end
junction cl.n0012 turns=custom
  stopline edge=cl.e0007 at=11.00
  connect from=cl.e0007:0 to=cl.e0011:0 turn=left protected=1

```

The node's plan is a library item, shared by every junction it suits:

```
ntw 1 signal id=lib.signal.4way_proleft
plan cycle=90.0 legs=4
phase p1 green=14.0 amber=3.0 allred=1.0 moves=n_lp,s_lp # protected lefts, north and south
phase p2 green=30.0 amber=4.0 allred=2.0 moves=n_s,n_r,n_l,s_s,s_r,s_l walk=e,w walktime=7.0 flash=12.0

```

Trunk roads live in `trunk/roads.ntw`, with bridges, ramps and interchanges authored as explicit edges and decks.
Districts join the trunk at named trunk nodes, so there is one owner per file and no shared edits.

### 4.6 The building library

Kinds of items, from small to large:
- **Kit pieces.** Furniture, fixtures, merchandise, doors, windows, stairs and railings. They are code-built meshes, as
  today: interiorfurniture.cpp, propmesh.cpp and interiorkit.cpp. Text refers to them by kind and parameters.
- **Furniture sets (`lib/sets`).** A hand-arranged group of kit pieces for one room type at one style or income level, for
  example "bedroom, retired couple, 1960s". The arrangement is relative to the room's anchor wall and door, and fits a
  range of room sizes.
- **Units (`lib/units`).** An apartment, office suite or hotel room. It has rooms (polygons), internal walls, doors, and
  furniture as explicit items or sets. It carries 2-4 furnishing variants, authored by hand: a, b, c.

  A unit fits a slot of a floor layout, and its outer walls are the slot's. The layout owns the exterior walls and their
  windows. The validator checks every unit a slot allows: each window must fall in one of the unit's rooms, and no
  bathroom gets a picture window.
- **Fit-outs (`lib/fitouts`).** A shop, restaurant, workshop or warehouse bay: the same model as a unit, plus counters,
  merchandise, staff and customer scenario points, and a sign slot.
- **Floor layouts (`lib/layouts`).** A floor plate:
  - exterior walls with their windows and doors;
  - cores (stairs, lifts, shafts);
  - corridors and common rooms;
  - unit or fit-out slots, as polygons with an entry door and a list of allowed units.
- **Plans (`lib/plans`):**
  - the footprint and massing;
  - the floor stack (which layout on which level, with levels and heights);
  - the facade style and its material slots;
  - the roof;
  - entrances;
  - optional features (porch, garage, balcony, awning, shutters);
  - default unit assignments.

One source of truth for windows: windows are openings in a layout's exterior walls. The compiler builds the facade from
the stacked layouts and the facade style: wall panels, window frames, glass, sills, lintels, belt courses at the floor
lines, cornices and the roof. So a window always belongs to the room behind it, and the validator checks the pairing
(section 9.1).

### 4.7 A placed building

### 4.8 Interiors in detail

Every floor of every building is a stacked layout. Here is how each part is represented:

| Part | Representation |
|---|---|
| Floors | `floor` records in the plan: level id, z, height; the layout used; floor and ceiling build-ups |
| Rooms | polygons (not just boxes as today), type, capacity, light schedule, ambient lux, daylight bounce, outdoor flag |
| Walls | segments with thickness; `ext=` marks exterior walls; finishes per side (from the room type, or overridden) |
| Doors | on a wall at a distance, with width, height, kind (hinged, pair, sliding, revolving, roll-up, lift), swing, style, which rooms they join, lock rules and public hours |
| Windows | on exterior walls: width, height, sill, kind, and the room they light. The facade is built from them |
| Stairs, lifts | cores in the layout that span named floor ranges: stair runs and landings, with their treads; lift shafts with cars, capacity, call points and stops. The compiler checks that the floors meet |
| Furniture | kit pieces and sets, positioned explicitly, with variants a/b/c authored by hand |
| Lights | fixtures with position, kind, schedule and animation (TV, neon, flicker), in the room they light |
| Collision | **derived** from the layout: walls, slabs, stair ramps, furniture boxes, door leaves (dynamic). Checked at compile time, built at load and cached. Per layout |
| Nav | **derived** at compile time and stored: a navmesh per layout, cut by walls and furniture, with off-mesh links for doors, stairs, lifts and seats. Per layout |
| NPC spots | scenario points as today's `InteriorScenario`, with the fields of outdoor spots (kind, capacity, hours, roles, prop, pose: section 12.4), plus markers (counter, bed, wardrobe, lift buttons) |

Because collision and nav are per layout, all instances of a layout share them, transformed at load. A midrise with six
identical typical floors has one navmesh and one collision set.

### 5.3 Authoring scripts

Allowed and encouraged, because their output is the static data. An agent runs a script once, reviews the result in the
previews, and commits the text. For example:

- `tools/author/street.py`: give it a block face, the district's plan list, and a rule such as "no plan twice within 5
  lots, no palette twice adjacent". It writes the building files for a row of lots with plans, mirrors, palettes and
  options chosen by a seed. It prints the seed, but the seed is not data: the output is.
- `tools/author/dress.py`: puts street trees every 12 m along an edge, lights every 30 m and bins at corners into the
  block file, as records that agents then move or delete.
- `tools/author/units.py`: fills a midrise's unit slots from the allowed lists with household mixes.

They are Python, run with `-I`, and never ship. The game doesn't know they exist.

## 6. Compiling and shipping

### 6.1 Text to binary: `worldc`

`tools/worldc.cpp` is a host tool, built and run by `build.sh` and `build.bat` before the game:
1. Parse all `world/**/*.ntw`, in parallel.
2. Validate them (section 9). The build stops on errors.
3. Resolve references, but keep them: plans and layouts are compiled once into the library section, and placements stay
   references with a configuration.
4. Derive the geometry of every library item and check it against the budgets:
   - exterior shells per plan and option set, at each level of detail (section 7.2);
   - room geometry and collision per layout, unit and fit-out.

   Meshes and collision are cheap to rebuild, so the game rebuilds them at load and caches them, and `world.bin` doesn't
   store them. Navmeshes are slow to build, so the compiler bakes one per layout, unit and fit-out and stores it.
5. Grade the terrain (section 3.3). The game computes the splat at load from the graded heights.
6. Quantise and pack into `world.bin`:
   - positions as cm in int32, delta-coded in polylines;
   - yaw as 16-bit;
   - IDs interned to 32-bit handles, with a string table for the names gameplay uses.
7. Compress each section with our own LZ codec:
   - an LZ4-class decoder is about 150 lines;
   - the encoder adds a hash chain;
   - decoding runs above 1 GB/s, so it never limits loading.
8. Write per-file content hashes, so only changed files are re-parsed and only dirty sections rebuilt. A one-building edit
   recompiles in under 2 s.

### 6.2 Size estimate

| Section | Content | Compiled | Compressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terrain | the baked grids (2.65 MB as NTG today), plus the grading as a delta; the splat is recomputed at load from heights and paint (~1 s) | ~20 MB | ~3-4 MB |
| Roads | ~10k edges, ~0.5M centreline points, junctions, signals, crossings, bays | ~6 MB | ~2 MB |
| Library | ~1,600 items: plans, layouts and placements of kit (~14 MB), plus a navmesh per layout, unit and fit-out (~6 MB); meshes and collision are rebuilt at load | ~20 MB | ~6.5 MB |
| Placements | 35,617 buildings × ~64 bytes, plus overrides (~10% of buildings × ~200 bytes) | ~3 MB | ~1.5 MB |
| Parcels and paths | ~45k polygons | ~4 MB | ~1.5 MB |
| Dressing | ~500k trees, props and lights × 12-16 bytes | ~7 MB | ~3.5 MB |
| Map | vector map; the base map texture as a 2560² palette image | ~8 MB | ~3 MB |
| **Total** | | **~70 MB** | **~20-30 MB** |

The text source in the repo comes to about 70 MB:
- library: 1,600 items, ~15 MB;
- placements: 35.6k buildings, ~18 MB;
- dressing: ~25 MB;
- roads, parcels and paths: ~11 MB.

### 6.3 Embed it in the exe (recommended), or ship a data pack

**Option A: embed `world.bin` in `NeonTide.exe` as an RCDATA resource.**

How:
- MSVC: `rc` with `world.rc`.
- MinGW: `windres`.
- At runtime: `FindResource` / `LoadResource` / `LockResource` hand us a pointer into the mapped image. There is no copy:
  the OS pages sections in as we touch them.

The exe grows from ~13 MB to ~35-45 MB, and the single-file distribution and the exe-only autoupdater stay exactly as they
are. CI adds `worldc` and the resource step to `build.bat`.

For development, `--worlddir=<path>` makes the game read `world.bin` from disk, or compile `world/` text directly in a
slower dev path. Iterating on data then needs no relink.

**Option B: `NeonTide.world` next to the exe.** It needs the autoupdater extended:
- a manifest with both hashes;
- download and verify both files;
- swap them atomically.

The exe would also need to refuse a mismatched pack. Only worth it if the compressed world passes ~200 MB, or if data
should ship more often than code.

**Recommendation: A.** It fits today's distribution and updater, and keeps the build reproducible: one commit gives one
exe. The data stays the same text in the repo either way, so moving to B later is a packaging change only.

## 7. Runtime

### 7.1 The switch and the loader

- `--world=hand|procedural`. Procedural stays the default until the hand world is playable (section 10).
- `World::HandWorld::load()` decompresses the sections. It builds the `WorldMap` from the baked and graded terrain, the
  `RoadNetwork`, a building index and the interior library, the transit net and the places. It then sets `gMap`, `gRoads`,
  `gBuildings` (or its successor), `gTransit` and `gInteriors`.
- Startup should drop from today's ~25 s of generation and map building (native) to ~4 s:
  - decompress the sections (~70 MB): under 0.1 s;
  - recompute the splat: ~1 s;
  - build indices and the lane graph: ~2-3 s (the lane graph could also be precompiled);
  - map textures: precompiled.

### 7.2 Exteriors and cell streaming

`generateCell(cx, cy, detail, out)` dispatches on the world kind. For the hand world it **instantiates** the cell from
compiled data. Nothing is invented:
- **Terrain patch and roads.** Today's road surface mesher (roadmesh.cpp: surfaces, markings, kerbs, sidewalks, junction
  boxes) runs on the authored network. Its random street dressing is switched off.
- **Buildings, in three levels of detail.** Each is compiled per plan and option set, and cached on first use: about 600
  plans × a few option sets.

  | Level | Range | Content | Budget | Drawn as |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Close detail | ≤ 100 m | window frames and sills in 3D, trims, gutters, porch railings, awnings, signs | 2-4k triangles per house | instanced per plan and option set, palette per instance: one draw per kind in range |
  | Near shell | ≤ 420 m | walls with the real window and door openings, flat window panels, roof, porch and garage masses | 1,000-1,500 triangles per house, ~10-30k per tower | merged into the cell mesh: one draw per cell, as today |
  | Far massing | ≤ 2.3 km | massing boxes; the facade shader draws the windows from each plan's real window rects | ~50-100 triangles per house | merged per cell, as today's far LOD |

  For the near shell and far massing, each building is transformed, mirrored (flipping the winding), tinted with its
  palette and appended to the cell's buffer. The vertex format stays unchanged (36 bytes). The tall landmarks stay in
  `siteFarCells`, as today.

  The cell census (section 13.3) sets the near shell's budget. Today a near cell averages 39k triangles (p99 144k, at
  most 171k), and a house costs about 2,400 triangles of its cell's mesh, roads included. Cells hold few buildings:
  Westbrook averages 42 per cell (at most 62), and the densest cell, in Calle Luna, holds 84. So a shell of 1,000-1,500
  triangles per house keeps the densest house cells under today's p99, and the finer detail comes only within 100 m.
- **Dressing.** Trees, props and lights come from the block files, placed as `PropInstance`s and `LightInstance`s. The
  instanced prop renderer is unchanged. Trees need instanced tree kinds; vegetation today is generated scatter.

**Windows before the interior is loaded.** A building's interior is not resident until you are close (section 7.4), so
its glass shows an *interior-mapped* room:
- a shader effect that fakes a room behind the glass, picked by the room type behind that window (bedroom, kitchen,
  office, shop);
- lit by that room's schedule.

The compiler bakes a small per-window record (room type, schedule, curtain), so a lit window at night is the room that is
really there. When a building's interior becomes resident:
- its windows switch to clear glass, through a per-building bit in a GPU buffer that the shell vertices index;
- the real rooms then show behind them.

This is new renderer work (decision D5). Today the facade shader already draws a window grid and lit storefronts, so
it is an extension of that.

### 7.3 Collision

**Exterior:** the shell per plan and option set (walls with door gaps, roof, porches) as boxes, built on first use and
instanced per placement into the cell's collision. That replaces today's one box per far building and the hollowed shells.

**Interior: per floor.** Each layout's collision (walls, slabs, stair ramps, furniture) is built on first use and cached
in layout space:
- it is added to the physics world when the floor loads, and removed when it unloads;
- door leaves stay dynamic colliders, as today;
- a building's floors above and below the loaded ones keep only their slabs and the shell.

### 7.4 Interiors at scale: streaming

**What is resident:**

| Trigger | Loads |
|---|---|
| On foot, within 35 m of a building's door or ground-floor window, and in the same or the facing block | that building's entry floor; the other floors too for houses (≤ 3 floors) |
| A door is used (the player, or a scripted scene) | that building at once, with priority. The door animation (0.4-0.6 s) covers the load |
| Inside a building | the player's floor, ±1 floor, and the entry floor |
| Walking up or down stairs | the next floor in the direction of travel, while on the stair |
| A lift is called | the destination floor, behind the closing doors and the ride (≥ 2 s), as today's express lift does |
| Looking through a window within 25 m (another building) | that floor's room on the window side, so you see the real room |
| Mission scripts | `holdInterior(id, floors)`, as today's `holdDef` |

**What is dropped:**
- buildings more than 60 m behind the player, with none of their doors in view;
- floors more than 2 away from the player's floor;
- least recently used first, when the resident budget is hit.

**Cost per floor** (loaded on a worker):
- instantiate the layout: transform its shared collision and nav; pick the furniture variants;
- build or fetch the layout's room mesh, which is cached per layout;
- spawn its NPCs (section 7.6).

| Budget | Target | Today, for scale |
|---|---|---|
| Resident buildings with interiors | ≤ 24 | 29 interiors exist; within 480 m all load |
| Resident floors | ≤ 48 | – |
| GPU memory for interiors | ≤ 384 MB: unique layout meshes are shared, furniture is instanced | 6k-142k triangles per interior today |
| CPU memory (collision, nav, NPC state) | ≤ 96 MB | – |
| Load time, first use of a layout | ≤ 30 ms on a worker | 5-260 ms per interior today, which builds every item mesh |
| Load time, layout already cached | ≤ 3 ms | – |
| Door-to-ready worst case | ≤ 150 ms, covered by the door animation | – |
| Triangles drawn per floor | ≤ 150k after room culling (the room and door graph) and furniture LOD; homes 15-40k, shops ≤ 140k with instanced merchandise; an office plate (tower floors average 1,485 m²) stores up to ~400k and draws the rooms in view | convenience stores 110-142k |

**Worst case, a tall tower.** 81 towers have 50 floors or more, and the tallest has 85:
- exterior: one cell mesh plus the skyline LOD;
- resident inside: the lobby, the player's floor ±1, and the lift destination. That is at most 4 floors, 4 × 150k
  triangles, ~60 MB;
- the other 81 floors exist only as slabs in the shell and as interior-mapped windows.

Its 85 floors reference perhaps 6 layouts:
- lobby;
- low, mid and high office plates;
- plant;
- penthouse.

So the tower's data is a few KB of references, and only the layouts touched are ever built. Lift rides stream the
destination floor. Stairs, used rarely in towers, stream the next floor while the player is on the flight.

### 7.5 Nav: per floor, joined to the street

**Roads.** `LaneGraph::build` runs unchanged on the authored `RoadNetwork`. Authored records override the derived ones
where they are written: lane changes and moves, stop lines, signal plans, turn rules, crossings, stops and kerbside
rules. Section 12 has the AI agent's requirements for them.

**Sidewalks and paths.** The walk graph is derived from the sidewalks, as today, plus authored `path` records (plazas,
parks, forecourts, through-block passages). That replaces `SiteSet::walks`.

**Buildings.** Each layout carries a navmesh with off-mesh links: doors, stair flights, lift stops and seats. Placement
adds the entrance links: from a building's exterior doors to the nearest walk node, compiled per placement.

**Paths.** Peds route on three levels:
1. walk graph to an entrance;
2. entrance to the floor's navmesh;
3. stairs or lifts to other floors.

Inside, only loaded floors have navmeshes, and the rest of the route is abstract (section 7.6).

### 7.6 NPCs inside 35k buildings without simulating them

1. **Abstract occupancy, for every building.** It is computed when needed, not ticked:
   - Each placement has an occupancy record: residents per unit, workers per office or shop, opening hours, household
     types.
   - A pure function `occupancy(building, unit, hour, day)` says who is home, at work, asleep or out.
   - It costs nothing for buildings nobody looks at.
2. **Materialise when a floor loads.**
   - Spawn the people the function says are there, at the scenario points of their rooms: sleeping in beds at 3 am,
     cooking at 7 pm, at desks at 10 am, staff behind counters during opening hours.
   - Identities are deterministic from the building, unit, day and slot, so a flat holds the same people when you come
     back.
3. **Fold back when the floor drops.**
   - Anything that changed is written to a small per-building delta that lives in the save for a few game days: someone
     killed, a door broken, a burglary reported, an alarm.
   - Everything else is forgotten, because the function regenerates it.
4. **Street to inside.** People entering a building on the street walk through its door (today's AI already uses doors).
   - If the floor is loaded they continue inside.
   - If not, they leave the simulation at the door and are counted in the building's "in".
   - People leaving buildings appear at doors, following the same schedules (rush hours, closing time).
5. **Police and events inside.**
   - Pursuits enter loaded floors through the navmesh.
   - Cops spawn at entrances.
   - Events (robberies, parties, deliveries) are anchored to a building's spots and fire only when it loads.
6. **Budget:**
   - ≤ 40 fully simulated indoor NPCs within 30 m;
   - others on loaded floors update at reduced rate;
   - zero cost for unloaded buildings.

## 9. Authoring tools

### 9.1 The validator (`tools/worldv.cpp`)

**Library checks.** These run once per item and are cached by content hash:
- Every room is reachable from an entrance, through doors and stairs or lifts, on every floor.
- Stairs and lifts connect the floors they claim. Landings, headroom and stops all line up.
- Every door joins two rooms, or a room and the outside.
- Walls don't cross. Rooms don't overlap, and every room is closed by walls or openings.
- Every window sits on an exterior wall of a room on its floor, with its `room=` right. Facade bays align with the
  windows of the floors above and below, or the plan says why not.
- Doors fit their walls, and door swings don't hit furniture.
- Furniture lies inside its room, doesn't overlap, and keeps a clear path from every door: navmesh connectivity per room.
- Collision and nav compile. Every scenario point is on the navmesh.

**Placement checks.** These run per building:
- Inside its parcel. No overlap with any other building, road, path or dressing collider.
- Its entrances reach a walk node within 30 m along paths.
- Its options and overrides name real elements.
- Its units fit their slots.

**Roads, walks and spots** (the AI agent's checks, section 12.6):
- the network is connected; every lane continues across every junction or ends at a legal dead end, and every approach
  has a movement out;
- moves match the lane counts;
- signal plans give every movement a green and every crossing a walk, and their cycles add up;
- crossings link both kerbs;
- bus stops are on lanes with a kerb, and parking bays don't overlap a nostop or a curb cut;
- every parcel with a driveway reaches a road;
- walk links don't end in walls, and no sidewalk runs over 250 m without a crossing;
- spots are neither inside geometry nor on a lane;
- every walkable surface has a level;
- a summary line, as today's load log: lanes, connectors, walk nodes and walk links, and the authored overrides by
  record type.

**Dressing:**
- nothing in drivable lanes or on doors and paths;
- everything on the ground.

**World:**
- IDs unique, references resolved;
- every place in `places.ntw` exists;
- transit stops are on their lanes.

**Speed at 35k buildings:**
- library: ~1,600 items × ~20 ms ≈ 30 s on one core, ~8 s on this machine's 4;
- placements: 35.6k × ~0.2 ms ≈ 7 s;
- roads and dressing: ~10 s;
- total under a minute for a full run on the dev box;
- **incremental mode** (only changed files and what references them): under 5 s, run before every commit.

### 9.2 Previews (host tools, PNG output, like today's citylab clay renders)

- **Top-down maps:** world, district or block at any scale. They show roads with lanes and markings, parcels, footprints
  coloured by plan family, paths, dressing and IDs on request.
- **Floor plans:** one PNG per floor of a layout, unit or placed building. They show walls, doors with swings, windows,
  room names and types, furniture, scenario points, stairs and lifts, the navmesh, and the validator's findings in red.
- **Cutaways:** 3D clay renders of a building with its floors stacked and peeled. They show facade and windows against
  the rooms behind them.
- **Contact sheets:** one per library category (all plans of a family, all units), for the variety review.
- **Street views:** clay renders at eye level along a block face, to spot visible twins before any game run.

### 9.3 Fast single-building loads

- `--world=hand --only=<building id>` loads the terrain patch, that building and its parcel, and places the camera at its
  door. That takes a second or two instead of a full world.
- `--viewer layout:<id>` and `--viewer plan:<id>` show a library item on a plinth, floor by floor, with the debug overlay
  (navmesh, collision, scenario points).
- A `worldc --check <file>` round trip of under a second, for authoring.

### 11.4 Risks

- **Effort.** Content authoring dominates, at 70% of the hours. Mitigations:
  - the street and dressing scripts;
  - the library's variant system;
  - previews that catch mistakes before game runs.
- **Repetition.** A library too small for its district shows. Mitigations:
  - the contact sheets and street views;
  - the "no visible twin" check in the validator, per block face;
  - adding plans to a family is cheap.
- **Interior streaming hitches.** Doors and lifts hide loads, and layouts are cached. The pilot measures worst cases.
- **Exterior memory.** Real windows on every facade cost triangles. Far LOD keeps the facade shader, and the near-cell
  budget (section 8) is enforced by the validator per cell.
- **Agent consistency at scale.** Mitigations:
  - one owner per area;
  - style guides per district (the plan families and palettes allowed);
  - the validator as the gate.

overrides.** The compiler and `LaneGraph::build` derive everything they derive today from the authored roads, paths and
buildings. A record is written only where a derivation would be wrong, or where nothing can derive the fact. The records
below are the ones docs/NTW_FORMAT.md freezes at v1, and src/world/ntwschema.cpp checks them.

### 12.2 Roads beyond the baseline

The baseline: nodes and edges with class, lanes per direction, lane width, medians, shoulders, parking, sidewalks and
speed; junction control; turn bans and lane connections; signalled and zebra crossings; bus stops, parking bays and curb
cuts; grade separation. Beyond it (4.4 shows them in a file):

1. **Lanes that change along an edge.** `lanes` gives one direction's count over a stretch, with the side that gains or
   loses the lane and the taper: turn pockets, merges, acceleration and deceleration lanes. `lane ... use=bus` keeps a
   lane for buses, streetcars or bikes. `noswitch` marks solid lines.
2. **Movements per lane.** `moves at=n1 lanes=l,s,sr` gives each lane's turns, from the left. The connectors are still
   generated from lane end to lane end, and `connect` overrides them for odd geometry.
3. **Priority without signals.** By road class, as today. Authored: `control=yield yield=<edges>`, `control=stop` with
   `stops=<edges>` for a two-way stop (all approaches without it), and `junction ... kind=roundabout` (yield at entry,
   circulating ring lanes).
4. **The junction box.** `stopline edge=... at=...` per approach, or the box polygon. The compiler turns either into
   today's cut-backs, and lanes start and end at the box, for the conflict test and "don't block the box".
5. **Signal plans** (kind `signal`, lib/signals). Phases by movement and by crossing, with green, amber and all-red,
   protected or permissive turns, and walk and flash times. A movement is the approach leg by compass and the turn
   (`n_l`; `n_lp` is a protected left); the compiler names the legs by bearing, and the node's `approaches` fixes skewed
   ones. `plan=auto`, the default, keeps today's planner. Signal heads are derived from the stop lines, and `head`
   overrides them.
6. **Highways.** Ramps are edges (`class=ramp`) with lane adds and drops. `shoulder=both:3.00` for breakdowns and
   police stops. `median=4.00:barrier`: no U-turns, no crossing on foot. Grade separation from `profile` and `deck`.
7. **Kerbside.** `parkbay` (parallel, angle or perpendicular, metered, disabled), `nostop` with its reason, `loading`
   with hours, `taxirank` with a capacity. Hydrants imply 5 m of nostop and curb cuts imply it across them. Police and
   cabs pull over on any kerb lane outside a nostop, with no record.
8. **Bus and streetcar stops.** `busstop` adds the waiting area (`wait=`), its `routes` and, for streetcars, the kerb
   lane kept clear (`clear=`).
9. **Off-street parking.** A `lot` block in the block file: entered by a curb cut, with bay `row`s and their aisles,
   `ramp`s between levels and a level per row.
10. **Dead ends.** `bulb=<radius>` on the node, or none, in which case long vehicles avoid it.
11. **z everywhere.** Every lane, connector and stop takes its z from the edge `profile` and `deck`. Lanes on a deck
    don't conflict with the lanes under it.

### 12.3 Sidewalks, crossings, jaywalking, frontage

1. **The walk graph** is derived from the sidewalks plus `path` records (7.5). Block files add `plaza` areas, where
   people mill about off the paths, and through-block passages (`path ... kind=alley`).
2. **Kerb side and building side.** Each sidewalk link keeps both, as the derived graph does today: panhandlers sit
   against the building side, shop windows face it, and people step back toward it from a car.
3. **Crossings** add `kind=unmarked` (no paint: people cross in gaps), and footbridges and underpasses as paths with a
   profile. Under `plan=auto`, the phase that gives a signal crossing its walk is derived from its approach, as today;
   an authored plan lists its walks.
4. **Jaywalking** keeps today's rule (narrow, slow, at least 12 m from a junction) on the authored widths and classes.
   `nojaywalk` marks fences and guard rails, and a median barrier implies it.
5. **Obstacles** (bins, lamps, planters, hydrants, newspaper boxes, signal poles, benches). Each catalog piece carries a
   simple collider (`collide=box|cylinder`), and walkers steer round them as today. The sidewalk's clear width is
   derived: its width less the furniture strip the placed props take.
6. **Frontage** is derived per placement by the compiler: windows with their unit's kind from the fit-out (bakery, bar,
   clothes), entrances with their public hours, and café terraces, kiosks, ATMs, vending machines and phone booths as
   spots.

### 12.4 Spots, zones and anchors

1. **Spots** are scenario points, outdoors in block files and indoors in layouts (4.5, 4.8): a kind, a position and yaw
   or a queue line, capacity, hours, shelter (`cover`, used in rain), who may use them (`roles`, `ages`), the prop and
   the pose. A queue's `for` names the door or counter it leads to. The kinds are listed in docs/NTW_FORMAT.md 9.5.
   Props imply the obvious ones (a bench prop is a bench spot), and the population's venues (3,955 laid out from site
   elements today) become spots and venues per block.
2. **Zones** replace `World::Region` for the AI: tagged polygons in the district file for the region (roles, density
   tables, local lines, the event mix, patrol density), gang turf, use (nightlife, beach, business, industrial,
   residential), and pedestrian and traffic density by hour and day. The audio and wildlife zones of 7.7 are the same
   set.
3. **Police anchors** in places.ntw: stations with their bays, garage exit, booking door and release point; K9 and SWAT
   bases; patrol loops, foot beats and break spots; helipads and boat launches; roadblock sites, which are derived at
   chokepoints as today, and authored where those read badly. Patrol density comes from police zones.
4. **Event anchors.** Every event kind keeps finding its own places on the graphs:
   - a 12 m kerb or shoulder stretch for crashes, breakdowns and traffic stops;
   - two lanes, or a 12 m/s road, with a kilometre ahead for racers;
   - a 4-way crossing of two ordinary streets, with lights or all-way stops, for takeovers;
   - a sidewalk or plaza point for the street events, and a 30 m sidewalk run for a shootout;
   - a building-side wall for the panhandler;
   - alleys, yards and car-park corners for the "wanted" kinds.

   `anchor kind=event` adds a place by hand.
5. **Emergency services:** ambulance bays and ER doors, fire stations and tow yards, as anchors.

### 12.5 Interiors (beyond 4.8, 7.5 and 7.6)

1. **Levels.** Every walkable surface has a level: outside, or a building and floor. The compiler assigns them to
   sidewalks, plazas, decks, garage levels and building floors, so none lacks one. A ped's level is then known without
   ray casts. The AI agent adds the level filter to its proximity queries (`pedsNear`, `vehiclesNear`, walk-graph
   lookups), and height queries answer per level.
2. **Stairs** are off-mesh links with rise, run and tread count (`stair ... treads=16 rise=0.18`; outdoor `steps` the
   same). Walkers climb a tread per step (about 0.5 m/s up and 0.55 m/s down) once the animator's slow-pace fix lands.
3. **Doors** carry their kind for the clip (hinged, pair, sliding, revolving, roll-up), lock rules with public hours,
   and a runtime state (locked, open, broken). Exterior doors join the walk graph (7.5), so today's street-door logic
   uses the real doors.
4. **Lifts** are off-mesh links with a car capacity, a call point and the stops. NPCs call, wait, ride and get out on
   their floor.
5. **Rooms** have a type and a capacity. Indoor spots take the outdoor kinds plus desk, counter service and queue,
   browse, bed, sofa, kitchen, washroom, bar and dance.
6. **Police and crime indoors.** Entry points are the exterior doors; exits and roof routes follow the doors, stairs and
   fire entrances; each shop's `counter` and back room (`room type=store`) serve hold-ups. The room-clearing order is
   derived from the door graph, and cover from the furniture, with `spot kind=cover` where that falls short.
7. **Occupancy** (7.6) also exposes the next transitions (who leaves for work at 8, who comes home at 6), so doors let
   people out and in at the right times, and per-building "in" and "out" counts for the crowds at entrances. Both are
   runtime calls, not data.

### 12.6 Validation

The validator (9.1) checks the AI's data:
- lanes that lead nowhere, and approaches with no movement out;
- moves that don't match the lane count;
- signal plans that never give a movement or a crossing its green;
- crossings with no walk phase;
- a bus stop on a lane with no kerb;
- parking bays overlapping a nostop or a curb cut;
- walk links that end in walls, and sidewalks with no crossing for over 250 m;
- spots inside geometry or on a lane;
- doors that join one room only;
- stairs and lifts that miss a floor;
- walkable surfaces without a level.

It ends with a summary like today's load log (`Lane graph: 25496 lanes, 56760 connectors, 40687 walk nodes, 56441 walk
links`), plus the count of authored overrides by record type, so a district's coverage shows at a glance.

### 12.7 What is derived, deferred or runtime

Nothing the AI asked for is dropped. These asks are met other than by a record:

| Ask | How it is met |
|---|---|
| Lot bays as `bays=row:...` on one line | a `lot` block with `row` and `ramp` records: the same data, one fact per line |
| Multi-level garages | in the format (levels, ramps). The pilot builds surface lots; garage interiors with ramps follow it |
| `moves dir=` | implied by `at`: the lanes arriving at `n1` are the `fwd` ones |
| Signal heads | derived from the stop lines; `head` where they don't suit |
| Signal movement names | `<approach>_<turn>`, compass approaches by bearing, `approaches=` for skewed junctions |

## 13. Prototypes run for this proposal

The prototypes are host tools in the city agent's scratchpad, not in the repo: `terrainbake.cpp`, and two query modes
added to a copy of `tools/worldcheck.cpp`. All ran on today's world (b71d339).

### 13.1 Terrain bake (`terrainbake.cpp`)

The prototype:
- ran the generator three times: natural terrain only (site layout skipped by a prototype switch), with the sites, and
  with the roads;
- diffed the three;
- wrote the bake files.

| Run | Time | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Natural terrain (site layout skipped) | 12.0 s | 183.5 km² of land, 235.9 km² of water; land spans x −9,644..6,124 and y −9,660..9,788 (15.8 × 19.5 km) |
| With the sites | 12.1 s | heights changed by more than 0.25 m on 3.73 km² (+9.1 to −15.3 m); 0.017 km² of land into water, 0.016 km² of water into land |
| Then the roads | 3.0 s | 6,223 nodes and 9,547 edges, as in the game; heights changed on 2.12 km² more (+91.8 to −17.5 m); no land into water, 0.041 km² of water into land |

Bake files (gzip -9 as a stand-in for our codec):

| File | Raw | Compressed |
|---|---|---|
| heights as float | 26.2 MB | 22.6-23.7 MB |
| heights in cm, int16 | 13.1 MB | 6.9 MB |
| heights in cm, int16, row-delta coded | 13.1 MB | 3.4 MB |
| water as float | 26.2 MB | 0.07 MB |
| water class, u8 | 6.6 MB | 0.04 MB |

What it shows:
- **The terrain separates.** With the site layout skipped, `WorldMap::generate` gives the natural terrain on its own, and
  the sites and the roads only edit it afterwards.
- **The edits are small.** The sites and the roads change heights on 5.9 km² together: 1.4% of the map and 3.2% of the
  land. Of that, the bake keeps only the port's landform; the pads and the road grading are re-authored. The sites move
  the coast on 0.033 km² in all.
- **The baked terrain is about 3.5 MB compressed**, once it is quantised to cm and row-delta coded. Floats barely
  compress. The real bake (stage 0, step 1) with our grid codec came to 2.65 MB for all four grids.
- **A rule for the compiler.** Today's road grading moves the coast in places (0.041 km²). The hand world's grading
  reports every texel it turns from water into land, and the validator rejects that unless the road or parcel asks for a
  fill (`fill=1`).

### 13.2 Building census (worldcheck `FP_STATS`)
